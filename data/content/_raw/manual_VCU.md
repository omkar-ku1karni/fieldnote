# Vantorr VCU Series Split-System Condensing Unit — Installation, Operation and Maintenance Manual

## 1. Safety considerations

### 1.1 Read before installation or service

This manual provides installation, operating, start-up, inspection, and service instructions for Vantorr VCU Series split-system condensing units. It applies to VCU-024, VCU-036, and VCU-048 equipment equipped with a single-stage scroll compressor and the VCB-110 control board. Keep this manual available at the installation site. Read the applicable sections before receiving the unit, connecting the refrigerant circuit, energizing electrical equipment, or opening a cabinet for service.

Installation and service must be performed by personnel trained to work with high-voltage electrical equipment and pressurized refrigeration systems. The installing contractor is responsible for checking the application, verifying the equipment identification, providing a safe installation, and confirming that the completed system operates as intended. These instructions do not replace site-specific safety procedures, applicable codes, or the requirements of the connected indoor equipment.

The safety notices used in this manual have the following meanings:

| Notice | Meaning | Required response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WARNING | A hazardous condition could cause serious injury or death. | Remove the hazard before proceeding. |
| CAUTION | A condition could damage equipment or cause improper operation. | Correct the condition before operation. |
| NOTE | Information needed to apply or interpret an instruction. | Read before performing the associated task. |

> **WARNING:** ELECTRICAL SHOCK HAZARD. Disconnect and lock out all electrical supplies before removing access panels or touching wiring, controls, motors, or terminals. A thermostat set to OFF does not establish an electrically safe work condition. Verify absence of voltage with a properly rated instrument.

> **WARNING:** REFRIGERANT PRESSURE HAZARD. R-410A equipment contains refrigerant under pressure. Never loosen a fitting, remove a component, or apply heat to a sealed refrigerant circuit before recovering refrigerant and verifying that the affected section is depressurized.

> **WARNING:** FIRE AND BURN HAZARD. Brazing produces hot surfaces and ignition sources. Keep combustible materials clear, provide suitable fire protection, and follow the established hot-work procedure. Do not direct flame toward valves, wiring, insulation, or the unit cabinet.

### 1.2 Personal protective measures

Wear eye and hand protection suitable for sheet metal, electrical inspection, refrigerant handling, and brazing. Use additional protective equipment appropriate to the task. Cabinet edges, tubing ends, and coil fins may be sharp. Surfaces near the compressor, refrigerant lines, and brazed joints may remain hot after operation. Assume moving fan components can restart whenever power is present and the control calls for operation.

Use a stable work platform with unobstructed access. Do not stand on the unit, use its panels as lifting points, or place loose objects near the fan discharge. Maintain clear communication among personnel working at the indoor and outdoor sections of the system. Before energizing the equipment, confirm that all tools, gauges, temporary jumpers, packaging materials, and unrelated personnel are clear of moving components.

### 1.3 Refrigerant handling

The VCU Series is designed for R-410A. Use only equipment, hoses, gauges, recovery cylinders, and service tools suitable for the refrigerant and operating pressures involved. Recover refrigerant using approved procedures; do not intentionally vent refrigerant. Prevent air, moisture, dirt, and incompatible substances from entering the circuit. Keep tubing ends capped until immediately before assembly.

Do not use oxygen or compressed air for pressure testing or purging the refrigerant circuit. Use dry nitrogen with a suitable regulator and equipment rated for the task. Follow applicable handling rules when transporting, recovering, storing, or reclaiming refrigerant. Keep recovery vessels away from excessive heat, and identify recovered refrigerant so that it is not mixed with unrelated material.

> **CAUTION:** Do not mix R-410A with any other refrigerant. Do not substitute a refrigerant based on apparent similarity or availability. An incorrect charge or contaminated circuit can cause unreliable operation and equipment damage.

### 1.4 Site and mechanical hazards

Secure the equipment against tipping, shifting, and vibration. Use lifting and support methods appropriate to the operating weight listed for the selected model. Protect nearby people and property while moving the cabinet. Inspect lifting equipment and supporting structures before the unit is placed. Never lift by refrigerant piping, service valves, panels, or electrical conduit.

Provide positive drainage around the outdoor base so that runoff cannot undermine the support or create an unsafe walking surface. Keep discharge and intake paths free of stored materials. Make provisions to prevent accidental contact with rotating equipment, hot surfaces, and energized parts during normal building use.

### 1.5 Stop-work conditions

Stop installation or service if the equipment identification is uncertain, the electrical supply does not match the unit rating plate, the piping cannot be installed within the stated limits, or the proposed location does not provide required access and airflow. Do not bypass a safety device to make the system run. Do not repeatedly reset a protective device without determining the cause of its operation.

If a procedure requires information not printed in this manual, consult the unit rating plate, the identified figure, the connected equipment instructions, or Vantorr Technical Support. Record the condition found and the corrective action taken before returning the system to service.

## 2. Model number nomenclature

### 2.1 Series identification

The VCU designation identifies a Vantorr split-system outdoor condensing unit. The model designation appears on the cabinet identification label and should agree with the equipment schedule, shipping documents, and service record. Use the complete model shown on the rating plate when identifying equipment. The three models covered here are VCU-024, VCU-036, and VCU-048.

| Designation | Description |
| --- | --- |
| VCU | Split-system condensing unit series |
| VCU-024 | Model covered by the VCU-024 specification and cooling charging chart |
| VCU-036 | Model covered by the VCU-036 specification and cooling charging chart |
| VCU-048 | Model covered by the VCU-048 specification and cooling charging chart |
| VCB-110 | Outdoor unit control board used in this series |

The model suffix distinguishes the applicable capacity class; it is not a substitute for confirming the actual electrical configuration. Each listed model has more than one voltage option. Read the rating plate on the delivered unit before selecting field wiring or overcurrent protection. Do not infer a factory-installed component, a field accessory, or a wiring configuration from the model suffix alone.

### 2.2 System identification at installation

The VCU is the outdoor portion of a split cooling system. Correct performance depends on the connected indoor coil, air-moving equipment, refrigerant piping, electrical supply, and controls. A Vantorr VAH air handler may be part of an applicable system, but the indoor equipment must be verified against the approved job documentation rather than assumed from the outdoor nameplate.

At receiving, record the outdoor model, complete rating-plate information, indoor equipment identification, installation location, and the responsible service contact. Keep those details with the building maintenance record. When ordering authorized replacements or requesting service guidance, provide the full equipment identification and a clear description of the observed condition.

> **NOTE:** The procedures in this manual apply to the identified VCU models. Where the rating plate, application documentation, or a named figure supplies a specific value, use that source rather than a value inferred from another model.

## 4. Location and clearances

### 4.1 Selecting the outdoor location

Locate the condensing unit where outdoor air can move freely through the coil and discharge away from the cabinet. Choose a location that limits recirculation of discharge air, provides practical access for service, and protects the unit from roof runoff, drainage problems, and avoidable physical damage. Consider occupant areas, equipment noise, neighboring equipment, windows, walkways, and future maintenance access before preparing the base.

Outdoor placement must permit safe routing of refrigerant tubing and electrical raceways without forcing tight bends or obstructing service access. Where several outdoor units are installed nearby, evaluate the combined airflow path instead of treating each cabinet as isolated. Screens, fences, storage enclosures, and decorative panels can interfere with airflow even when they appear to leave space around the unit.

### 4.2 Clearance verification

The required clearances for every model are identified in the applicable specification table. Identify the service side by the actual cabinet access arrangement before positioning the equipment. Measure the available space from the closest permanent obstruction, not from a proposed landscaping line. The top discharge area must remain unobstructed.

| Location feature | Field check |
| --- | --- |
| Service side | Confirm the access panel can be removed and service tools can be used. |
| Other cabinet sides | Check for walls, screens, stored items, and adjacent equipment. |
| Top discharge | Confirm a clear vertical discharge path. |
| Refrigerant connection area | Leave room to support and service tubing without damaging the cabinet. |
| Electrical access | Maintain safe entry and isolation access for service. |
| Drainage path | Direct water away from the base and occupied walking surfaces. |

If a proposed placement cannot meet the published requirements, revise the location before installing the unit. Do not treat removable panels, a narrow gap beside a wall, or a future promise to clear storage as a substitute for permanent working space.

### 4.3 Base and attachment

Install the unit on a stable, level support capable of carrying the equipment and resisting expected site forces. Use a suitable outdoor pad or designed structural platform. Check for settlement risk, frost effects, standing water, and local requirements for anchorage. Support the cabinet at its intended base points and avoid loads that distort the frame or cause the service panels to bind.

For elevated or roof-mounted installations, confirm structural capacity and safe access with the responsible project personnel. Provide vibration isolation where required by the approved design, without allowing the cabinet to shift or piping to carry mechanical loads. Arrange tubing supports so that building movement and normal equipment vibration do not pull against service connections.

### 4.4 Weather, debris, and public access

Keep leaves, grass clippings, dust-producing operations, drifting snow, and windborne debris away from the coil. Do not place the unit directly below a location that continuously drains onto electrical components or builds ice around the base. Protect the cabinet from impact where service vehicles, carts, or pedestrian traffic may reach it. If a protective barrier is installed, it must not reduce the required clearances or interfere with airflow.

Inspect the area after installation work by other trades. New walls, planting, fencing, exhaust outlets, or rooftop equipment can change the airflow conditions that existed at start-up. Record any arrangement that could affect operation and correct it before occupancy or turnover.

> **WARNING:** Do not operate a condensing unit with its top discharge obstructed or with access panels removed. Airflow restriction and exposure to moving or energized parts create unsafe operating conditions.

### 4.5 Placement acceptance procedure

1. Confirm the model and the installation position against the job documents.
1. Inspect the support for level, stability, drainage, and adequate load capacity.
1. Verify the service side and all required cabinet and top clearances.
1. Check that the air path will remain clear after nearby work is completed.
1. Verify that refrigerant lines and conduits can reach the unit without damage or obstruction.
1. Secure the cabinet as required by the approved site design.
1. Record the final location and any site protection provisions in the commissioning record.

## 5. Refrigerant piping

### 5.1 Piping design and limits

Refrigerant piping links the outdoor condensing unit to the approved indoor evaporator section. Route the piping to minimize unnecessary length, abrasion, exposure to damage, and inaccessible joints. The maximum total refrigerant line length is 100 ft, with a maximum vertical lift of 40 ft.

Measure the actual installed route, including offsets and changes in elevation. Confirm that both total length and vertical separation remain within the published limits before cutting tubing. Do not assume that a route is acceptable because the units are close together in a straight line. If a longer or more complicated route is proposed, stop and obtain project-specific application direction rather than extrapolating beyond this manual.

Use refrigerant-grade tubing appropriate for the application and the approved indoor equipment. Select line diameters and routing details from the approved system design and applicable equipment instructions; this manual does not authorize a generic diameter selection. Protect tubing from kinks, vibration damage, moisture entry, and direct contact with sharp metal edges. Provide insulation and weather protection suitable for the refrigerant line and installation environment.

### 5.2 Receiving and protecting the refrigerant circuit

Inspect the outdoor connections and factory service valves for shipping damage before field piping begins. Verify that the system components match the equipment schedule and are intended for R-410A service. Keep all openings sealed until the associated joint is ready to be made. Never use tubing that is visibly crushed, contaminated, or deformed at the connection surface.

> **CAUTION:** Moisture and dirt left in a refrigeration circuit can impair refrigerant flow and damage components. Keep the system clean and dry during assembly. A successful vacuum reading does not excuse poor handling or an uncorrected leak.

### 5.3 Tubing support and routing

Provide independent supports for long horizontal runs, vertical risers, and any point where tubing could rub against building structure. Allow for thermal movement and normal equipment vibration. Avoid rigidly clamping both lines to surfaces that transmit objectionable noise to occupied spaces. At wall and roof penetrations, install suitable sleeves and seals while protecting insulation and tubing from damage.

Do not bury an unprotected service joint in a wall or ceiling cavity. Arrange connections and service access so that a technician can inspect them later. Keep tubing clear of electrical terminals, rotating fan components, and hot or abrasive surfaces. Protect exposed insulation from sunlight and weather in a manner suitable for the site.

### 5.4 Brazing and joint assembly

Use established refrigeration brazing practices and compatible materials. Prepare each joint by cutting the tubing cleanly, removing burrs without introducing debris, and cleaning mating surfaces. Arrange the assembly without forcing the piping into alignment. Support the joint so that heating and cooling do not put a bending load on the service valve or indoor connection.

1. Verify that the section being worked on is safe to open and free of trapped refrigerant.
1. Position fire protection and move combustible material away from the heating area.
1. Remove or shield nearby insulation, controls, wiring, and cabinet surfaces as necessary.
1. Establish a controlled dry-nitrogen purge through the tubing while brazing.
1. Heat the joint evenly and apply the appropriate brazing filler in accordance with the material instructions.
1. Allow the joint to cool without disturbing alignment or imposing a mechanical load.
1. Inspect the completed joint and remove residue that could interfere with inspection.
1. Restore insulation and protective coverings only after testing has established joint integrity.

> **WARNING:** Never braze a closed, pressurized section of refrigerant piping. Recover refrigerant and verify depressurization before heating any connection that may contain refrigerant.

### 5.5 Pressure testing and leak inspection

After piping is assembled, follow an appropriate pressure test procedure for the connected equipment and refrigerant system. Use dry nitrogen and a regulated test source. Obtain the applicable test limits from the approved system documentation and component ratings. Do not guess a test pressure from the refrigerant type, and never exceed the rating of the weakest component in the test section.

Isolate or protect components as required by the approved procedure. Inspect all field joints, service connections, and accessible circuit areas with a method suitable for refrigeration service. If a leak is detected, depressurize safely, repair the joint, and repeat the test. Do not use refrigerant charge as a substitute for confirming a sound piping installation.

### 5.6 Evacuation and dehydration

Once pressure testing is complete, remove the test gas by an approved method and evacuate the field-installed circuit with clean, properly maintained vacuum equipment. Use suitable evacuation connections that do not unnecessarily restrict flow. Measure the condition of the system with an instrument intended for evacuation verification, rather than relying only on the running condition of the vacuum pump.

Follow the evacuation completion and standing-test criteria in the approved service procedure. If the system does not hold the required condition, investigate possible leakage, trapped moisture, and service-connection problems. Correct the cause and repeat the process. Keep the circuit isolated from outdoor air when hoses or instruments are removed.

> **NOTE:** Do not open the factory refrigerant charge to field piping until the installed circuit has passed leak inspection and evacuation. Follow the service-valve operating instructions provided for the installed equipment.

### 5.7 Final piping inspection

Before start-up, confirm that tubing is secure, insulated where required, protected at penetrations, and clear of sharp edges. Check that service-valve access is practical and valve caps are properly installed after service. Confirm that no tubing or hose remains in contact with a fan guard or panel. Record the actual piping route, installed length, elevation relationship, leak-test completion, and evacuation acceptance in the service record.

## 6. Long line sets

### 6.1 Determine whether long-line guidance applies

Line length and elevation affect the refrigerant inventory and operating behavior of a split system. Evaluate the installed route before releasing the factory charge or adding refrigerant. Factory charge covers the standard line set length of 25 ft.

A system with piping beyond the standard covered length may require an adjustment to the refrigerant charge. The amount must be determined from the Vantorr figure provided for this series, not from an assumed charge-per-foot rule. The refrigerant addition indicated by the figure is part of the overall commissioning evaluation; it does not replace the model-specific cooling charging chart.

The VCU Series Additional Refrigerant for Long Line Sets figure is used to determine the prescribed line-set charge adjustment; locate the installed piping condition using the figure's labels and read the corresponding instruction or amount directly from the figure.

[[FIGURE:VCU-LS]]

### 6.2 Long-line review procedure

1. Confirm the complete outdoor and indoor equipment identification.
1. Measure and document the installed refrigerant line route and elevation change.
1. Verify that the application remains within the stated overall length and lift limits.
1. Compare the measured condition with the VCU Series Additional Refrigerant for Long Line Sets figure.
1. Record the figure-directed charge adjustment without substituting an estimated quantity.
1. Complete the piping integrity test and evacuation procedure before admitting refrigerant.
1. Account for the prescribed adjustment during charging, then verify operating conditions with the applicable model charging chart.
1. Document any discrepancy between calculated charge requirements and observed operating conditions for investigation.

### 6.3 Installation considerations for extended runs

Avoid unnecessary elbows, unsupported risers, poorly insulated sections, and concealed connections that cannot be serviced. Extended tubing routes can make troubleshooting harder because a problem at the indoor coil, a hidden joint, or an intermediate support may be far from the outdoor cabinet. Label the piping where it crosses mechanical rooms or areas shared with other equipment.

Protect the suction line insulation against gaps, compression, and water intrusion. Insulation that becomes wet or displaced can lead to condensation on surrounding finishes and affect the operating condition of the system. Do not allow a liquid line to rub through its protective covering against building metal. Confirm that the route permits inspection where practical.

> **CAUTION:** Do not extrapolate the additional-refrigerant figure beyond its presented range. Do not assume that an adjustment appropriate to one VCU model or line arrangement is valid for another.

### 6.4 Conditions requiring additional review

If the proposed route exceeds a published limit, includes an unusual elevation arrangement, or cannot be reconciled with the figure, do not proceed on the assumption that charge adjustment alone makes the design acceptable. Escalate the application for review through the responsible designer or Vantorr Technical Support. Provide the model, indoor equipment identification, measured route, installation conditions, and relevant observations.

When a line set has been modified after initial start-up, treat the work as a change to the refrigerant system. Review the installed length again, repeat applicable integrity and evacuation checks, and reassess the charge using the long-line figure and the corresponding cooling chart. Update the system service record so future technicians know that the piping arrangement has changed.

Electrical installation must be performed by qualified personnel in accordance with applicable codes and the approved equipment documentation. The available voltage options are listed under Specifications, but the delivered unit rating plate governs the actual power supply configuration. Verify the building supply against the installed unit before making a connection. Do not assume that an outdoor cabinet can be converted between supply configurations in the field.

Provide an accessible means of disconnecting power and appropriate circuit protection for the specific unit. Select conductors, raceways, grounding methods, disconnect equipment, and protective devices from the rating plate, the approved design, and the electrical data figure. Never substitute a familiar value from another installation.

> **WARNING:** HIGH VOLTAGE. More than one electrical supply may be associated with the complete split system. Isolate the outdoor unit and connected indoor equipment as required before working on control or refrigerant-system components. Confirm all supplies are de-energized.

The VCB-110 coordinates the outdoor unit's response to a cooling demand and the applicable protective inputs. The complete split system also depends on an appropriate indoor control arrangement. Confirm the compatibility of the thermostat, indoor equipment, and field control circuit against the wiring diagram and approved system documentation.

Route control conductors separately from sources of electrical noise where required by the design and installation rules. Protect wiring against sharp cabinet edges and exposure to water. Do not bypass a protective input, bridge unknown connections, or apply an external signal to a board point without verifying its intended purpose in the diagram. An apparent absence of response may represent a normal operating delay or protective condition rather than a failed control board.

## 8. Outdoor air sensor check

### 8.1 Purpose and inspection

The outdoor air sensor provides a temperature-related input for the equipment control system. An inaccurate reading can lead to unexpected control behavior or an incorrect service conclusion. Evaluate the sensor and its circuit before replacing the control board or assuming that the temperature reported by the system is correct.

Inspect the sensor mounting location and exposed wiring for mechanical damage, looseness, water intrusion, and evidence of improper handling. The sensing element should be exposed to the intended outdoor air condition rather than directly influenced by an abnormal heat source or trapped discharge air. Correct obvious placement or connection problems before interpreting an electrical measurement.

> **WARNING:** Disconnect power and establish an electrically safe condition before disconnecting or checking a sensor circuit. Do not perform resistance measurements on an energized circuit.

### 8.2 Resistance-to-temperature reference

The VCU Series Sensor Resistance vs. Temperature figure is used to evaluate the outdoor sensor against its temperature-dependent resistance curve; measure the sensor's actual surrounding temperature, then find that temperature on the figure and read the corresponding resistance from the curve.

[[FIGURE:VCU-TH]]

Compare the independently measured sensor condition with the figure, allowing for measurement quality and the conditions at the sensor itself. Do not substitute a room thermostat reading, a weather report, or air temperature measured at a distant location for the temperature directly surrounding the sensor. For a sensor near a heat source, first correct the exposure or allow the element to reflect the actual outdoor condition.

### 8.3 Sensor verification procedure

1. Record the outdoor operating conditions and the observed symptom.
1. De-energize the equipment and verify an electrically safe condition.
1. Inspect the sensor body, mounting position, and accessible wiring.
1. Disconnect or isolate the sensor as required to obtain a valid resistance measurement without parallel circuit influence.
1. Measure the temperature adjacent to the sensor with a suitable independent instrument.
1. Measure the sensor resistance with a suitable meter.
1. Compare the measured pair to the VCU Series Sensor Resistance vs. Temperature figure.
1. Recheck the measurement setup and circuit condition if the result is inconsistent.
1. Restore the sensor connection, secure the wiring, and verify the system response after reassembly.

### 8.4 Interpreting an unexpected result

If the reading differs from the figure, consider poor contact at the measurement points, damaged wiring, a connector problem, moisture intrusion, or a sensor that has not reached the surrounding air condition. A measurement taken through a connected control circuit may be misleading. Perform the test as directed, isolate the element when appropriate, and confirm that the instrument is functioning correctly.

A sensor that measures consistently with the figure does not by itself establish that the control board receives the correct input. Inspect the complete circuit and interpret any reported diagnostic status with the LED figure. Do not condemn an electronic component based on an assumed resistance value or an undocumented temperature conversion.

> **NOTE:** Use the supplied resistance curve for this series. Other sensing elements may have different characteristics even if they look alike or fit the same mounting location.

## 9. Sequence of operation

### 9.1 Cooling demand

The VCU Series provides outdoor refrigerant condensing and compression for a compatible split cooling system. With proper power, control signals, and allowable operating conditions present, a request for cooling is processed by the VCB-110. The control coordinates the outdoor operating sequence while the connected indoor equipment provides the appropriate evaporator airflow and indoor-side operation.

At a valid cooling demand, the control evaluates its operating conditions and protective inputs. When operation is permitted, the outdoor compressor and fan operate as required for the cooling cycle. Refrigerant circulates through the connected indoor and outdoor components, transferring heat from the conditioned space to the outdoor air. The single-stage scroll compressor operates as an on/off cooling component rather than a variable-capacity stage commanded by this manual.

The indoor air-moving system must be running as required by the approved control arrangement. Lack of suitable indoor airflow can result in poor cooling, abnormal refrigerant behavior, and protective operation. The outdoor compressor should never be used as proof that the indoor side is functioning correctly.

### 9.2 Demand satisfied

When the cooling demand is removed, the control ends the outdoor cooling operation according to its normal logic. The indoor equipment may respond according to its own control instructions. Do not infer a fault solely because indoor and outdoor components do not stop at the same observable instant; first verify the intended sequence for the complete system.

Following a stop, the outdoor control applies an anti-short-cycle delay of 5 minutes before permitting a subsequent compressor start. This delay helps avoid immediate restart after a brief interruption or changed demand. A thermostat adjustment made during the delay does not override it.

> **NOTE:** If a cooling request is present but the compressor does not start immediately, check for the anti-short-cycle delay before diagnosing a failure. The absence of immediate compressor operation does not establish a defective contactor or control board.

### 9.3 Protective operation

The VCB-110 monitors applicable inputs and can prevent or interrupt normal operation when a condition requires protection. Protective operation must be investigated before repeated restart attempts. The specific status indication and its interpretation are provided in the Status LED Flash Codes figure; do not substitute an informal flash-count rule.

When a protective condition is suspected, document the demand state, outdoor condition, recent service work, and the board's observed status. Verify supply integrity, airflow, condenser cleanliness, refrigerant circuit condition, and related components in a safe order. The correct response is to address the underlying condition rather than defeating the protective circuit.

### 9.4 Ambient conditions and system response

Each model has a listed minimum outdoor temperature for cooling. Confirm the prevailing outdoor condition before requesting operation for commissioning, troubleshooting, or occupied building use. Operating outside the stated cooling application limit requires separate approved application direction and is not authorized by this manual.

A cooling call does not guarantee that all components will operate if the system is being held off by a required delay, an interlock, or a protective condition. A technician should evaluate the entire command path, from the space controller and indoor equipment through the outdoor VCB-110, without assuming that any individual device is at fault.

### 9.5 Normal observations

During stable operation, the outdoor fan should move air through the condenser without obstruction, the compressor should run without unusual mechanical noise, and the cabinet should remain secure. Observe the connected indoor system for airflow and cooling performance. Verify actual refrigerant behavior with the appropriate charging chart rather than with sound, line temperature by touch, or the mere presence of cooling at a supply outlet.

If vibration, noise, intermittent operation, frost, or inadequate cooling is observed, perform the troubleshooting checks in this manual. Never reach into an energized fan area to locate a noise. Shut down and isolate the equipment before touching any moving or exposed component.

## 10. Start-up

### 10.1 Conditions before energizing

The start-up inspection establishes that the outdoor unit, the indoor section, and field-installed systems are ready for operation. Commissioning should be performed after construction work that could contaminate coils, restrict filters, damage wiring, or obstruct airflow has been completed. Confirm that the installation has passed the relevant mechanical, refrigerant, and electrical inspections.

Complete the following review before energizing:

| System area | Required pre-start condition |
| --- | --- |
| Identification | Outdoor and indoor equipment match the approved system selection. |
| Cabinet | Unit is level, secured, clean, and fully assembled. |
| Airflow | Outdoor clearances are present; indoor airflow path is ready. |
| Piping | Correct route, support, insulation, and accessible service connections. |
| Refrigerant circuit | Leak inspection and evacuation are completed. |
| Electrical system | Supply, grounding, protection, and controls are verified. |
| Sensors | Accessible sensor wiring and mounting are secure. |
| Documentation | Model charts, wiring figure, and service record are available. |

> **WARNING:** Do not energize equipment with cabinet panels missing, loose electrical conductors, exposed moving parts, or an unverified refrigerant circuit. Correct unsafe conditions before commissioning.

### 10.2 Outdoor unit inspection

Remove shipping material, loose packaging, and construction debris from the cabinet and coil area. Inspect the base and mounting arrangement for stability. Check that refrigerant tubing is supported independently and cannot transmit an excessive load to the cabinet. Confirm that the fan can rotate freely with power isolated and that no foreign object is present near the fan path.

Inspect the coil without forcing tools through the fins. If dirt or debris is present, clean it using the maintenance guidance before first operation. Verify that service panels fit correctly and that all intended access covers can be secured. Restore any protective material removed for brazing or wiring work.

### 10.3 Refrigerant system readiness

Verify that leak testing and evacuation were successfully completed, that service valves are in the correct operating position, and that no temporary shipping closure remains in the intended flow path. Confirm the installed line length and elevation conditions against Sections 5 and 6. Document the original charge basis and any additional refrigerant introduced under the long-line guidance.

Do not attempt to judge charge from compressor sound or the apparent coldness of a pipe. The final operating charge must be evaluated under the conditions and instructions of the appropriate model charging chart. If ambient or indoor conditions do not permit a valid chart comparison, record that limitation and complete the charge verification when eligible operating conditions are available.

### 10.4 Indoor system readiness

Check the connected indoor air handler or other approved indoor assembly using its own installation instructions. Confirm that the coil is clean, filters are installed and unobstructed, the condensate path is functional, and the blower and controls are ready for a cooling request. A restriction or missing component indoors can produce an outdoor symptom that resembles a refrigerant problem.

Coordinate with building personnel before initiating the test. If occupied spaces are served, advise the responsible contact that the cooling system will be run and observed. Keep access to the indoor and outdoor units available until testing is complete.

### 10.5 Initial energization and demand

1. Confirm that all service work is complete and all required covers are installed.
1. Verify the correct electrical supply and disconnect arrangement against the unit rating plate.
1. Restore electrical power in accordance with the approved commissioning procedure.
1. Observe the VCB-110 diagnostic indication and consult the Status LED Flash Codes figure as needed.
1. Place the connected indoor control in a condition that requests cooling.
1. Confirm that the indoor equipment responds as intended by its own control sequence.
1. Observe the outdoor equipment for an allowed start, accounting for the anti-short-cycle delay.
1. Check for abnormal noise, cabinet movement, loss of airflow, and other signs requiring immediate shutdown.
1. Allow operating conditions to stabilize sufficiently for the applicable charging-chart procedure.
1. Record measured operating conditions and complete the refrigerant verification.

If the unit does not start, do not bypass a safety device or force a contactor closed to complete commissioning. Check for a valid call, correct power, the scheduled control delay, and the board indication using the appropriate figure. If the observed condition cannot be resolved safely, leave the unit out of service.

### 10.6 Operating evaluation

Observe the system through a normal cooling demand and a satisfied demand. Confirm that the outdoor fan has a clear air path and that the compressor does not produce unusual mechanical noise. Verify that indoor air movement and condensate removal are functioning. Check for vibration-related contact between tubing and structure, and for moisture forming where insulation is missing or damaged.

Review refrigerant performance according to the applicable model chart. Use calibrated instruments and record the conditions under which readings were taken. Measurements made while the unit is starting, cycling, or subject to abnormal airflow may not represent the steady operating condition needed for a valid charging decision.

### 10.7 Shutdown and restart observation

Remove the cooling demand and confirm that the outdoor equipment responds appropriately. After the unit has stopped, establish another demand only as needed for the commissioning check and observe that the control honors its anti-short-cycle protection. Do not repeatedly cycle power in an effort to accelerate the test. Complete any required observations without overriding the intended control logic.

If an unexpected protective indication appears, preserve the observed status and investigate. Record both the original symptom and the final operating result. If the unit cannot be returned to normal service, identify it clearly for building personnel and describe the condition requiring further correction.

### 10.8 Commissioning record

| Record item | Entry to document |
| --- | --- |
| Outdoor identification | Complete model and rating-plate information |
| Indoor identification | Installed indoor unit and coil identification |
| Site | Location, support arrangement, and access conditions |
| Refrigerant piping | Installed length, elevation relationship, and test results |
| Electrical | Supply verification and inspection outcome |
| Controls | Demand response and diagnostic observations |
| Refrigerant | Chart used, measured conditions, and final charge record |
| Turnover | Technician, date, open items, and building contact |

Give the owner or designated maintenance contact a copy of the completed record. Explain the location of disconnects, basic filter and coil maintenance responsibilities, warning indications that require service, and the importance of keeping outdoor airflow clear.

## 11. Refrigerant charging

### 11.1 General charging requirements

Use R-410A and equipment rated for the refrigeration system. Confirm airflow, coil condition, service-valve position, ambient conditions, and stable cooling operation before adjusting charge. A pressure reading taken under abnormal conditions may lead to an incorrect diagnosis. Recover refrigerant using approved procedures and introduce or remove charge in a controlled manner. Document the final quantity and the reason for any adjustment.

Use the cooling chart for the exact model installed. Read the complete chart, including its labels and notes, before choosing a target or deciding whether a reading is applicable. Account for the line-set requirements from Section 6. Do not infer a target pressure or charging correction from another VCU model.

> **CAUTION:** Do not charge a system that has an unresolved leak or unverified indoor airflow. Repair the defect and establish proper operation before evaluating refrigerant charge.

### 11.2 Charging the VCU-024

Confirm the VCU-024 model identification and use the corresponding factory charge listed in Specifications as the equipment reference. Review the completed line-set record and any figure-directed adjustment. Confirm that cooling operation is stable and that the installed indoor equipment is operating normally.

The VCU-024 Cooling Charging Chart is used to verify charging conditions for this model; identify the applicable operating condition on the chart, follow its printed reading method, and compare the measured condition with the chart's indicated target.

[[FIGURE:VCU-024-CC]]

Make only corrections supported by the chart and the verified system condition. Recheck operation after a correction and record the result. If the observed readings cannot be reconciled with the chart, investigate airflow, coil cleanliness, instruments, piping, and refrigerant circuit integrity before changing charge again.

### 11.3 Charging the VCU-036

Verify that the cabinet identification reads VCU-036. Check the piping record and confirm that any required long-line adjustment has been evaluated. Confirm that the indoor section is prepared for the test and the system is operating without an unresolved protective condition.

The VCU-036 Cooling Charging Chart is used to verify charging conditions for this model; locate the applicable operating condition using the chart headings and follow the figure's prescribed method to read and compare its charging target.

[[FIGURE:VCU-036-CC]]

Do not apply the VCU-024 or VCU-048 chart. If a measured condition conflicts with the expected result, verify the instrument connections and operating state before adding or recovering refrigerant. Record the chart reference, observations, corrective action, and final verification.

### 11.4 Charging the VCU-048

Confirm the VCU-048 model and the matching indoor system. Review line length, elevation, service work, leak-test results, and the charge history. Start the system under eligible cooling conditions, confirm proper airflow, and obtain a stable operating condition before measuring.

The VCU-048 Cooling Charging Chart is used to evaluate charging conditions for this model; find the applicable operating condition on the figure and read the indicated charging target according to the labels and notes shown.

[[FIGURE:VCU-048-CC]]

Compare the actual system readings with the chart rather than relying on a generic charging rule. Make a controlled correction only when the full installation condition supports it. Finish with a repeat operating check and a documented charge record.

## 12. Control board diagnostics

### 12.1 VCB-110 status indication

The VCB-110 provides a status indication for evaluating outdoor unit operation. Interpret the observed indication only with the figure for this series. Record what the indicator displays before interrupting power or changing the call for cooling; an altered operating state may remove useful evidence.

The VCU Series Status LED Flash Codes figure is used to interpret VCB-110 status indications; observe the indicator, locate the matching displayed pattern in the figure, and read the associated instruction from the figure.

[[FIGURE:VCU-LED]]

> **WARNING:** Live electrical diagnosis may expose energized components. Only qualified personnel using appropriate protective practices may perform measurements requiring the equipment to remain energized.

### 12.2 Diagnostic procedure

1. Record the cooling demand, system response, and current diagnostic indication.
1. Compare the indication with the VCU Series Status LED Flash Codes figure.
1. Review the indicated checks and inspect the corresponding system condition.
1. Verify power, control connections, airflow, and relevant protective circuits as applicable.
1. Correct the identified cause without bypassing a safety device.
1. Restore covers and wiring before requesting another normal operating cycle.
1. Confirm the unit operates correctly and document the final status.

If a board indication does not match an available figure entry, verify that the board is the VCB-110 and that the correct equipment documentation is being used. Contact Vantorr Technical Support with the observed symptom and model information if clarification is needed. Do not replace the board solely because an unfamiliar indication appears.

## 13. Maintenance

### 13.1 Maintenance responsibilities

Periodic inspection preserves airflow, identifies damage, and helps prevent avoidable outages. Establish an inspection schedule appropriate to site debris, weather exposure, operating duty, and service history. Shorten the interval when conditions produce heavy coil contamination or repeated airflow restrictions. Work must be performed by qualified personnel when electrical access or refrigerant-system service is involved.

> **WARNING:** Disconnect and lock out power before cleaning, opening panels, or touching internal components. Verify that the fan has stopped and that the work area is safe.

### 13.2 Maintenance checklist

| Component or area | Inspection | Corrective action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoor coil | Dirt, debris, bent fins, restricted air path | Clean carefully and correct blockage. |
| Cabinet and base | Corrosion, damage, loose mounting, poor drainage | Repair, secure, and restore drainage. |
| Fan area | Debris, rubbing, unusual noise, loose guards | Remove obstruction and investigate damage. |
| Refrigerant piping | Oil staining, rubbing, insulation damage, unsupported runs | Investigate leaks; restore support and insulation. |
| Electrical compartment | Moisture, damaged wiring, loose connections | Correct with power isolated. |
| Sensor | Exposure, mounting, connection condition | Correct mounting and test against sensor figure. |
| Indoor equipment | Filters, coil condition, drainage, airflow restrictions | Service per indoor equipment instructions. |
| Service records | Repeat trips, repairs, charging history | Review trends and schedule needed work. |

### 13.3 Coil and cabinet cleaning

Remove loose surface debris without pressing it deeper into the coil. Use a cleaning method and product appropriate for the coil materials, and protect electrical components from water. Avoid high-force cleaning that bends fins or damages tubing. Restore the original airflow path by clearing debris from around the base and nearby barriers.

Inspect panels and seals after cleaning. Replace damaged protection before energizing the unit. Check for new storage or landscaping that reduces the specified clearances. Maintain a clear path for service and top discharge throughout the operating season.

### 13.4 Refrigerant and electrical inspection

Look for signs of leakage without disturbing an intact circuit unnecessarily. If a leak is suspected, use appropriate detection procedures, recover refrigerant when repairs require opening the circuit, and repeat applicable pressure testing and evacuation. Do not routinely add refrigerant to compensate for an unresolved loss.

With power safely isolated, inspect accessible wiring and control components for discoloration, moisture, rubbing, and insecure connections. Restore enclosure protection and verify that conduit entry points remain secure. Use the electrical figure and rating plate for any service requiring identification of circuit components or protective equipment.

### 13.5 Return to service

After maintenance, remove tools and cleaning materials, secure all panels, restore power, and request normal operation. Observe the fan and compressor for unusual noise or vibration. Confirm indoor airflow and review any VCB-110 diagnostic indication through the LED figure. Record findings, corrective work, and outstanding concerns in the maintenance log.

## 14. Troubleshooting

### 14.1 Approach

Begin with the reported symptom and verify it under safe operating conditions. Confirm model identification, cooling demand, outdoor ambient condition, supply configuration, and relevant service history. Do not replace a major component before checking the installation conditions that could cause the same symptom. When measurements depend on a figure, use the applicable VCU figure rather than a remembered value.

> **WARNING:** Never defeat an electrical or refrigerant protective device to keep the compressor operating. Stop if diagnosis requires an unsafe connection, an unverified pressure condition, or access to moving components.

### 14.2 Troubleshooting table

| Symptom | Possible cause | Check or corrective action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit does not start on cooling demand | No valid request from indoor control | Confirm indoor control operation and the approved control circuit. |
| Unit does not start on cooling demand | Required restart delay | Allow the specified anti-short-cycle delay and observe again. |
| Outdoor equipment remains off | Supply or disconnect problem | Verify rated supply and disconnect condition safely. |
| Outdoor equipment remains off | Protective control condition | Observe VCB-110 status; see the Status LED Flash Codes figure. |
| Compressor runs but cooling is weak | Restricted indoor airflow | Inspect filters, indoor coil, blower operation, and duct restrictions. |
| Compressor runs but cooling is weak | Outdoor coil restriction | Clean the condenser coil and restore clearances. |
| Cooling is inadequate | Incorrect refrigerant charge | Check for leaks and use the exact model cooling charging chart. |
| Cooling is inadequate | Installation or piping issue | Confirm line route, insulation, and long-line figure requirements. |
| Equipment cycles unexpectedly | Intermittent demand or control issue | Review control request, connections, and diagnostic indication. |
| Equipment cycles unexpectedly | Airflow or refrigerant problem | Check coil cleanliness and verify operating conditions. |
| Excessive vibration or noise | Cabinet, fan, piping, or mounting interference | De-energize; inspect support, clearance, and mechanical contact. |
| Frost or abnormal condensation | Airflow or refrigerant circuit problem | Inspect indoor equipment and evaluate refrigerant system safely. |
| Outdoor sensor reading appears wrong | Sensor exposure or circuit problem | Check placement, wiring, and sensor resistance curve. |
| Repeated protective operation | Underlying system fault remains | Document indication, correct cause, and retest without bypassing protection. |

### 14.3 Verify the correction

After corrective work, restore all connections and panels, establish normal cooling operation, and confirm that the original symptom no longer occurs. Verify the refrigerant condition with the correct model chart when the repair affected system performance or charge. Repeat the operating sequence test where a control fault was involved.

If symptoms persist, compare the observed condition with the wiring figure, sensor curve, LED figure, and charging chart in the order relevant to the fault. Record actual observations and the tests already completed before contacting Vantorr Technical Support. Provide the complete unit identification, connected indoor equipment, operating conditions, and a concise description of the unresolved issue.

> **NOTE:** A successful restart alone does not establish that a recurring fault has been corrected. Verify stable operation and document the cause found before closing the service record.
