## 1. Safety considerations (A2L)

### 1.1 Flammability Classification and General Safety Precautions
The Vantorr VRL Series commercial packaged rooftop unit utilizes R-454B refrigerant, which is classified under ASHRAE Standard 34 as an A2L mildly flammable refrigerant. While A2L refrigerants exhibit significantly lower flammability risks compared to higher-flammability hydrocarbons, strictly enforced safety protocols are mandatory during installation, commissioning, servicing, and decommission activities.

All service technicians working on VRL Series units must hold valid certification in A2L refrigerant safety handling and recovery procedures. Installation and service work must comply fully with all applicable local, state, and national building, electrical, mechanical, and fire codes.

> **WARNING:** Risk of fire or explosion. R-454B is an A2L refrigerant with a low burning velocity. Do not use open flames, unrated electrical switches, or non-spark-proof tools within the immediate work area when opening the refrigeration circuit or servicing components containing refrigerant. Ensure adequate natural or mechanical ventilation is maintained throughout all installation and service procedures.

> **WARNING:** Disconnect all electrical power sources to the unit, including indoor blower disconnects and auxiliary control circuits, before removing any service access panels. Ensure all electrical power is locked out and tagged out in accordance with Standard OSHA Lockout/Tagout procedures. Multiple disconnect switches may be required to completely de-energize the unit.

> **CAUTION:** The VRL Series unit contains pressurized A2L refrigerant and moving mechanical components. Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including safety glasses with side shields, cut-resistant gloves, protective footwear, and thermal protection when handling liquid refrigerant lines or working near sheet metal edges.

> **NOTE:** Store all installation materials, tools, and replacement components in a dry, covered area prior to installation. Retain this manual with the rooftop unit at all times for future technician reference.

### 1.2 Leak Detection and Work Area Monitoring
Before opening any portion of the sealed refrigeration system or performing any brazing or cutting operations on refrigerant tubing, the work area must be continuously monitored using a calibrated, spark-proof electronic leak detector designed specifically for A2L fluorinated olefines.

- Verify the work space is free from potential ignition sources, including spark-producing tools, static discharge risks, open flames, hot surfaces, and non-explosion-proof electrical devices.
- Place a portable A2L-certified refrigerant detector at floor level or low-lying areas near the unit base, as R-454B vapor is heavier than air and can accumulate in confined or poorly ventilated areas.
- Maintain a minimum 10-foot radius clear zone around the rooftop unit and open access doors during line evacuation, charging, or refrigerant recovery operations.
- Ensure a dry chemical (Class ABC) or carbon dioxide (CO2) fire extinguisher is readily accessible within the immediate work zone before opening any service valves or brazing ports.

### 1.3 Safe Handling, Purging, and Recovery Procedures
When evacuating, recovering, or charging R-454B refrigerant in VRL Series rooftop units:

1. Never use oxygen or compressed atmospheric air for leak testing or pressure testing system piping. Use only dry nitrogen mixed with a trace amount of R-454B if required by local codes, or pure dry nitrogen for standard pressure decay testing.
2. Evacuate systems using recovery machines specifically rated and certified for A2L mildly flammable refrigerants. Non-sparking recovery equipment featuring brushless DC motors and sealed relay enclosures must be employed.
3. Prior to applying heat for unbrazing or component removal, recover all existing refrigerant charge to an approved A2L recovery cylinder. Purge the entire refrigeration circuit with dry nitrogen at low pressure for a minimum of 5 minutes to sweep out residual hydrocarbon vapors.
4. Ensure recovery cylinders are approved for R-454B (DOT 4BA or 4BW specification), are equipped with pressure relief valves, and feature left-hand threaded ports where required by regulation.
5. Do not vent A2L refrigerants to the atmosphere. Always recover refrigerant in accordance with local environmental regulations.

## 4. Rigging and roof curb

### 4.1 Unit Inspection and Handling
Upon receiving the unit at the job site, inspect all outer crating and equipment panels for evidence of shipping damage. Check unit rating plate data to confirm the model, voltage, and phase match job site electrical specifications. Report any shipping damage or component shortages to the carrier immediately.

- Keep the unit upright during moving and rigging operations.
- Leave protective crating and corner covers in place until the unit is lifted into position on the roof curb.
- Verify that the crane operator and rigging crew are informed of the total operating weight of the unit before hoisting.

### 4.2 Lifting and Spreader Bar Requirements
Rigging of VRL Series rooftop units requires a minimum of four lifting cables attached to the designated lifting lugs on the unit base frame. 

> **WARNING:** Heavy equipment hazard. Failure to use proper lifting equipment and spreader bars can result in severe structural damage to cabinet panels, internal coils, or fatal personal injury. Spreader bars must be wider than the overall cabinet width to prevent cables from crushing unit side panels.

1. Attach lifting shackles or hooks exclusively to the factory-provided rigging holes in the base rails.
2. Install spreader bars above the unit cabinet top. Spreader bars must exceed the unit cabinet width by at least 6 inches on each side to prevent rigging cables from bearing against side panels or fan guards.
3. Maintain a minimum lifting cable angle of 60 degrees relative to horizontal to distribute loads evenly.
4. Test-lift the unit a few inches above the transport bed to verify center of gravity and stability prior to hoisting onto the building roof curb.

### 4.3 Roof Curb Installation and Sealing
Roof curbs must be assembled, leveled, and flashed in accordance with local building codes and National Roofing Contractors Association (NRCA) guidelines prior to setting the VRL unit.

- The roof curb must provide continuous perimeter support along the entire base rail of the rooftop unit.
- Verify the curb is square by measuring diagonal dimensions across opposite corners. Diagonal measurements must match within 1/4 inch.
- Level the curb across all four sides. The maximum allowable pitch out-of-level in any direction is 1/16 inch per foot of curb length.
- Apply closed-cell perimeter gasket material (minimum 3/8-inch thick by 1-inch wide) continuously along the top mating flange of the roof curb prior to landing the unit.
- Ensure all duct connections (supply and return air drops) are securely attached to the roof curb internal supports and insulated before lowering the unit onto the gasketed curb perimeter.

## 5. Clearances

Adequate clearance around the unit is essential to ensure proper air circulation through the condenser coils, provide sufficient space for routine service and component replacement, and maintain safe working conditions for field technicians.

> **CAUTION:** Inadequate clearances around the condenser section will cause recirculation of warm discharge air, leading to elevated head pressures, reduced cooling capacity, and potential high-pressure compressor trips.

Required minimum clearances for VRL Series units are as follows:

- **Service Side Clearance**: 48 inches must be provided on the main electrical and compressor access side of the unit to permit removal of access doors, electrical testing, blower assembly servicing, and compressor maintenance.
- **Condenser Side Clearance**: 36 inches must be maintained around all condenser coil faces to allow unrestricted outdoor air intake and fan air discharge.
- **Top Clearance**: 72 inches unobstructed space must be preserved directly above the top of the unit discharge fan guard. Do not locate units beneath overhangs, structural ledges, or trees that restrict vertical discharge airflow.
- **Bottom Clearance**: Continuous perimeter curb fitment or minimum clearance as dictated by roof mounting guidelines.

## 6. Condensate

The VRL Series unit features a sloped primary condensate drain pan constructed of corrosion-resistant stainless steel or engineered polymer. Proper condensate drain line installation is necessary to prevent water backup, panel corrosion, and indoor air quality issues.

### 6.1 Drain Trap Sizing and Pitch Requirements
Because the indoor blower operates upstream or downstream of the evaporator coil creating negative static pressure in the drain compartment during operation, an external P-trap must be field installed on the condensate outlet connection.

- The primary condensate drain connection is a 3/4-inch female NPT fitting located on the side panel near the evaporator section.
- Install a cleanout tee immediately downstream of the unit connection before the trap bend to facilitate seasonal flushing and maintenance.
- Construct the P-trap using rigid PVC or copper pipe. The vertical drop from the bottom of the drain pan connection to the top of the trap seal must be at least 3 inches, and the trap seal depth must be at least 2 inches plus the total negative static pressure of the system in inches water column (in. w.c.).
- Pitch the horizontal drain line away from the unit at a slope of no less than 1/8 inch per foot toward an approved roof drain or condensate disposal system.

```
   Unit Drain Outlet
         |
         v
       +---+
       |   |  <- Cleanout Cap (Tee)
       |   +------------------------------------+
       |                                        |  Slope 1/8" per foot ->
       +-------+                                +----------------------> Drain
               |                                |
               |        A (Trap Depth)          |
               +--------------------------------+
```

### 6.2 Secondary Drain Connections and Winterization
- If required by local building codes, connect a secondary overflow drain line to the auxiliary condensate outlet. Route this line to a conspicuous location to alert building management of primary drain blockage.
- In freezing climates, wrap exposed condensate piping located above the roofline with self-regulating freeze protection heat trace cables rated for outdoor commercial use, or drain the trap completely during cold weather shut-down periods.

## 7. Electrical and three-phase power (wiring figure)

### 7.1 Field Wiring and Power Supply Requirements
All electrical wiring must be installed in accordance with the National Electrical Code (NEC), NFPA 70, local electrical codes, and manufacturer instructions. Field power must be supplied through a dedicated, fused disconnect switch or circuit breaker installed within line of sight of the rooftop unit.

- Inspect the unit rating plate for Minimum Circuit Ampacity (MCA) and Maximum Overcurrent Protection (MOP) values. Conductors must be sized based on MCA ratings, taking into account ambient temperature derating factors and line voltage drop across long wire runs.
- Bring incoming three-phase field wiring into the unit main electrical control box through the factory-provided knockout locations on the base rail or cabinet side. Use liquid-tight flexible metal conduit and weather-tight conduit hubs to preserve cabinet rain tightness.
- Connect line-voltage power leads to the main terminal block or disconnect terminals provided in the control compartment. Connect the primary equipment grounding conductor firmly to the ground lug marked `GND`.

> **WARNING:** Risk of electrical shock and equipment destruction. High voltage is present at line connections. Ensure main disconnect is open and locked out before wiring. Verify phase-to-phase and phase-to-ground voltages match rating plate parameters before applying power.

### 7.2 Three-Phase Rotation and Phase Monitor Operation
VRL Series rooftop units contain two tandem scroll compressors and variable indoor blower motors that require proper three-phase rotation. Operating scroll compressors in reverse rotation for extended periods will cause severe mechanical damage, high operating noise, and immediate pump failure.

1. Before starting the compressors, verify incoming phase rotation using a calibrated three-phase rotation meter connected to incoming terminals L1, L2, and L3. Standard rotation is A-B-C (L1-L2-L3).
2. The factory-installed phase monitor inside the VCB-400 electrical enclosure checks incoming voltage for proper phase sequence, phase loss, and line voltage imbalance.
3. If the phase monitor detects improper rotation or phase loss upon initial power-up, the VCB-400 control board will prevent compressor contactors from energizing.
4. If phase rotation is incorrect, open the field disconnect switch, lock out power, and swap any two incoming main power leads at the disconnect load terminals. Re-apply power and confirm normal phase monitor status.

## 8. Crankcase heaters and pre-start

### 8.1 Crankcase Heater Purpose and Pre-Start Requirement
VRL Series units feature belly-band electrical crankcase heaters wrapped around the lower shell of both tandem scroll compressors. The crankcase heater prevents liquid R-454B refrigerant from migrating to and condensing within the compressor oil sump during off cycles. 

Liquid refrigerant diluted in compressor lubricant causes severe liquid slugging, loss of oil viscosity, bearing failure, and compressor damage upon start-up.

- Crankcase heaters must be energized at least 24 hours before the first start-up.
- Ensure main line voltage power is applied to the unit disconnect switch with all thermostat cooling calls (Y1, Y2) disabled during this initial 24-hour warm-up period.
- Verify visually and thermally that both crankcase heaters are drawing current and warming the bottom shell of each compressor prior to initiating compressor operation.

### 8.2 Pre-Start Visual and Mechanical Inspection
Before applying power for initial start-up or seasonal commissioning, complete the following mechanical checks:

1. Confirm all shipping brackets, wood skids, and blower transit bolts have been completely removed.
2. Inspect the indoor blower wheel and motor assembly. Rotate the blower wheel manually to ensure smooth rotation without binding or rubbing against the housing.
3. Check tension and alignment on all belt-drive pulley systems (if equipped). Belt deflection should measure approximately 1/2 inch at the midpoint of the belt span under moderate thumb pressure.
4. Inspect all refrigeration piping connections, TXV bulbs, and liquid line sight glasses for signs of oil stains, which indicate shipping leaks.
5. Verify that return air filters are clean, dry, correctly sized, and oriented with airflow arrows pointing toward the indoor coil.

## 9. Economizer (general operation, changeover setpoint is field adjustable)

### 9.1 Economizer Overview and Operation
The optional factory-installed or field-fitted economizer damper assembly provides energy-efficient free cooling by utilizing cool outdoor air to satisfy space cooling demands instead of operating mechanical refrigeration circuits.

- The economizer assembly consists of outdoor air and return air dampers linked to a fully modulating 24VAC spring-return actuator managed by the VCB-400 main control board.
- When outdoor air conditions are suitable for free cooling, the control board modulates the outdoor air damper open while closing the return air damper to maintain supply air temperature setpoints.
- Minimum outdoor air positions can be configured on the VCB-400 board to satisfy local ventilation codes during occupied hours.

### 9.2 Changeover Setpoint Adjustment
- Economizer operation is controlled by a dry-bulb changeover setpoint that is field adjustable.
- The field-adjustable changeover dial or configuration setting on the economizer module allows technicians to select the outdoor air temperature threshold above which economizer cooling is disabled and mechanical cooling is enabled.
- To adjust the changeover threshold, locate the economizer setting potentiometer or display interface on the economizer module inside the return air compartment. Rotate the dial or adjust the digital setpoint to match local climate conditions and building design specifications.

> **NOTE:** If outdoor air dry-bulb temperature exceeds the selected changeover setpoint, the economizer damper returns to its minimum ventilation position, and the unit transitions automatically to two-stage mechanical cooling as called for by space thermostat inputs (Y1, Y2).

## 10. Sequence of operation — two-stage cooling

The VRL Series unit control system relies on the VCB-400 main control board to manage indoor fan operation, economizer dampers, safety interlocks, and two-stage tandem scroll compressor operation across two independent refrigeration circuits (Circuit 1 and Circuit 2).

```
Thermostat Demand Logic Flow:
[ Thermostat G Signal ] --------> [ Indoor Blower Enters Continuous Operation ]
[ Thermostat Y1 Signal ] -------> [ Economizer / Stage 1 Compressor Circuit 1 ]
[ Thermostat Y2 Signal ] -------> [ Stage 2 Compressor Circuit 2 Energized ]
```

### 10.1 Fan Operation (G Signal)
When the space thermostat calls for continuous indoor fan operation by energizing terminal `G`, the VCB-400 control board energizes the indoor blower contactor or ECM control output. 

- The indoor blower motor starts and runs at configured continuous fan speed.
- If an economizer is installed, the outdoor air damper opens to the field-selected minimum ventilation position.

### 10.2 First-Stage Cooling Operation (Y1 Signal)
Upon a call for first-stage cooling from the space thermostat (energizing terminal `Y1`):

1. The VCB-400 control board checks all system safety interlocks, including low-pressure switches, high-pressure switches, and refrigerant detection system status.
2. The board verifies that the internal 5-minute anti-short-cycle delay timer has expired.
3. If outdoor ambient conditions are suitable for free cooling, the economizer modulates outdoor dampers to satisfy cooling demand.
4. If outdoor ambient conditions are above the field-adjustable dry-bulb changeover setpoint, the board energizes compressor contactor CC1.
5. Compressor 1 on Circuit 1 starts immediately, providing 50% nominal mechanical cooling capacity. The indoor blower operates at first-stage cooling airflow speed.
6. Condenser fan contactors energize to maintain stable liquid head pressure across Circuit 1.

### 10.3 Second-Stage Cooling Operation (Y2 Signal)
If space temperature continues to rise and the thermostat energizes terminal `Y2`:

1. The VCB-400 control board verifies that Compressor 1 has been operating for the required minimum runtime and confirms that Circuit 2 safety interlocks are closed.
2. The board energizes compressor contactor CC2.
3. Compressor 2 on Circuit 2 starts immediately, bringing the rooftop unit to 100% full nominal cooling capacity (both Circuit 1 and Circuit 2 fully active).
4. All outdoor condenser fans operate at maximum speed to reject heat from both refrigerant circuits.
5. The indoor blower ramps to full second-stage design airflow.

### 10.4 Satisfied Thermostat Signals and De-energization
- When second-stage cooling demand is satisfied, terminal `Y2` de-energizes. The VCB-400 opens contactor CC2, de-energizing Compressor 2 on Circuit 2 after completing minimum run-time requirements.
- When space temperature reaches the first-stage setpoint, terminal `Y1` de-energizes. The board opens contactor CC1, stopping Compressor 1 on Circuit 1 and initiating the 5-minute anti-short-cycle delay timer.
- If terminal `G` remains energized, the indoor blower continues to operate. If `G` de-energizes, the indoor blower stops after a short fan off-delay.

## 11. Start-up checklist

Perform the following verification steps prior to and during system start-up. Check each item upon successful completion.

### 11.1 Pre-Start Physical Check
- Unit is installed level on roof curb with approved perimeter gasket seal.
- Required clearances verified: service side 48 in., condenser side 36 in., top 72 in. unobstructed.
- Ductwork connections securely sealed and insulated.
- Primary condensate drain trap installed with proper seal depth and pitched 1/8 in. per foot.
- Return air filters installed:
  - VRL-090: 4 filters, 20 x 25 x 2 in.
  - VRL-120: 4 filters, 24 x 24 x 2 in.
  - VRL-150: 6 filters, 24 x 24 x 2 in.
- Shipping hardware and blower transit brackets removed.

### 11.2 Electrical and Power Check
- Field wiring matches line voltage rating plate (208/230-3-60 or 460-3-60).
- Ground conductor attached securely to grounding terminal `GND`.
- Control transformer primary tap set correctly for system voltage (208V vs 230V).
- Three-phase power phase sequence verified A-B-C at incoming line terminals.
- Crankcase heaters energized for a minimum of 24 hours prior to compressor start-up.

### 11.3 Operational Startup
- Apply thermostat cooling call Y1. Confirm Compressor 1 and outdoor fan start smoothly.
- Apply thermostat cooling call Y2. Confirm Compressor 2 starts smoothly.
- Verify indoor blower motor amperage draw does not exceed motor rating plate full-load amps.
- Inspect liquid line sight glasses on both circuits for clear liquid flow.
- Check economizer damper movement and verify field-adjustable dry-bulb changeover setpoint.
- Complete refrigerant charging performance check using model-specific charging charts.

## 12. Refrigerant charging (both circuits; one subsection per model with its charging chart)

### 12.1 General Charging Guidelines for R-454B
VRL Series units are factory charged with R-454B refrigerant. Circuit 1 and Circuit 2 are separate, independent refrigeration systems containing thermal expansion valves (TXVs) and liquid line filter driers.

> **WARNING:** Risk of flammability and high pressure. R-454B is an A2L refrigerant. Always charge liquid R-454B slowly into the liquid line service port or suction line with a liquid-charging adapter when compressors are operating. Never liquid-charge directly into the suction service valve without a throttling manifold valve to prevent compressor liquid slugging.

- Verify factory charge values on the unit rating plate prior to adding refrigerant:
  - VRL-090: Factory refrigerant charge is 10 lb 1 oz.
  - VRL-120: Factory refrigerant charge is 12 lb 11 oz.
  - VRL-150: Factory refrigerant charge is 15 lb 5 oz.
- Minimum outdoor temperature for cooling operation during charging is 40 °F.
- Connect precision digital pressure gauges and thermistors to the liquid and suction service valves of the circuit being tested.
- Operate the unit at full capacity (Y1 and Y2 active) for a minimum of 15 minutes to allow system operating pressures and subcooling to stabilize before taking readings.

### 12.2 Charging the VRL-090
Refer to the VRL-090 Cooling Charging Chart to determine the correct liquid pressure for each refrigeration circuit.

The VRL-090 Cooling Charging Chart plots liquid pressure at the service valve (vertical axis) against suction pressure at the service valve (horizontal axis). Each sloped line represents one outdoor ambient dry-bulb temperature entering the condenser coil. To use the chart, find the measured suction pressure on the horizontal axis, move up to the line for the current outdoor temperature (interpolating between lines when needed), and read the target liquid pressure on the vertical axis.

[[FIGURE:VRL-090-CC]]

1. Connect the gauge manifold to the Circuit 1 liquid and suction service valves. Operate both cooling stages (Y1 and Y2) for at least 15 minutes.
2. Measure the outdoor ambient dry-bulb temperature entering the condenser coil.
3. Find the measured Circuit 1 suction pressure on the horizontal axis of the VRL-090 Cooling Charging Chart and read the target liquid pressure on the line for the measured outdoor temperature.
4. If the measured liquid pressure is below the target, add R-454B slowly as liquid. If it is above the target, recover refrigerant into an approved A2L recovery cylinder. Charge is correct when the measured liquid pressure is within the tolerance printed on the chart.
5. Move the gauges to Circuit 2 and repeat the procedure.

## 13. Indoor airflow

Correct indoor airflow across the evaporator coil is required for rated cooling capacity, to prevent coil icing, and to keep the charging charts valid. Set indoor airflow before charging.

### 13.1 Determining airflow
Each model's Indoor Blower Performance table lists delivered airflow (CFM) for each blower speed tap (rows) at external static pressures from 0.1 to 1.0 in. w.c. (columns).

1. Measure total external static pressure (ESP) in in. w.c. with readings taken in the supply and return ducts near the unit casing.
2. Identify the blower speed tap currently selected.
3. In the Indoor Blower Performance table for the model, find the row for that speed tap and the column for the measured external static pressure.
4. Read the delivered airflow at the intersection. Interpolate between columns when the measured static pressure falls between them.
5. If airflow is outside the design range, select a different speed tap and repeat the measurement.

## 14. Refrigerant detection system (A2L)

### 14.1 System Purpose and Component Location
VRL Series units operating with A2L refrigerant R-454B are equipped with a factory-installed Refrigerant Detection System (RDS) fully integrated with the VCB-400 control board.

- The system consists of an A2L refrigerant leak sensor mounted in the evaporator fan compartment beneath the indoor cooling coil, where leaking refrigerant would naturally settle.
- An auxiliary leak sensor is installed in the return air plenum to monitor return airstreams.
- The RDS system continuously monitors indoor cabinet air for concentrations of R-454B vapor.

### 14.2 Automatic Mitigation Sequence
If the RDS sensor detects R-454B refrigerant concentration approaching safety thresholds:

1. The VCB-400 control board immediately trips into Mitigation Mode.
2. Compressor contactors CC1 and CC2 are instantly de-energized, stopping both refrigeration circuits.
3. The indoor blower motor is forced to run continuously at 100% full capacity to disperse and dilute accumulated refrigerant vapor throughout the building duct system.
4. If an economizer is present, outdoor air dampers modulate to 100% open position to exhaust indoor air and draw in fresh ambient air.
5. Alarm outputs on the VCB-400 board energize, signaling an A2L alarm condition to building management systems.
6. Mitigation mode remains active until refrigerant concentration levels drop safely below detection thresholds and the system is manually reset or auto-cleared per board sequence.

> **WARNING:** Risk of explosion or toxic gas accumulation. If the RDS mitigation mode activates, do not enter the mechanical room or roof area with open flames or non-rated electrical equipment. Locate and repair the refrigerant leak using an A2L-approved leak detector before resetting system power.

## 16. Maintenance

Regular preventive maintenance is necessary to ensure high operating efficiency, extend equipment life, and prevent unexpected system lockouts. Maintenance should be performed semi-annually by qualified service technicians from Ridgeline Mechanical Services or an authorized commercial HVAC contractor.

> **WARNING:** Risk of electrical shock and moving mechanical parts. Turn off all electrical power disconnect switches before removing service doors or performing maintenance on blower wheels, belts, or electrical components.

### 16.1 Maintenance Schedule Summary

| Component | Maintenance Activity | Recommended Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return Air Filters | Inspect filters; replace if dirty or damaged | Monthly to Quarterly |
| Evaporator Coil | Inspect coil face; clean with non-acidic cleaner | Annually (Spring) |
| Condenser Coils | Clean outdoor coils; clear debris and leaves | Annually (Spring/Summer) |
| Condensate Drain | Clean drain pan; flush P-trap cleanout | Bi-annually (Spring/Fall) |
| Indoor Fan / Belts | Inspect drive belt tension, sheaves, motor bearings | Bi-annually |
| Electrical Contacts | Check contactor tips, wire terminal torque | Annually |
| Crankcase Heaters | Verify electrical current draw and heater operation | Annually (Before Fall) |
| A2L Leak Sensors | Perform RDS sensor self-test and visual inspection | Annually |

### 16.2 Air Filter Replacement
- Open filter access panel on the return air section.
- Remove dirty return air filters:
  - VRL-090 requires 4 filters, 20 x 25 x 2 in.
  - VRL-120 requires 4 filters, 24 x 24 x 2 in.
  - VRL-150 requires 6 filters, 24 x 24 x 2 in.
- Install replacement MERV-rated commercial filters of matching dimensions, ensuring airflow direction arrows point toward the indoor evaporator coil.

### 16.3 Coil Cleaning Procedures
1. **Condenser Coil**: Wash outdoor coils with low-pressure water spray directed from the inside of the coil outward to push dirt away from fins. Do not use high-pressure power washers as fan fins can bend easily.
2. **Evaporator Coil**: Apply an A2L-safe, non-acidic foaming coil cleaner approved for indoor use. Flush thoroughly with fresh water and confirm condensate pan drains freely during cleaning.
