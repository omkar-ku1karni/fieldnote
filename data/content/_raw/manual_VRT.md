# Vantorr VRT Series Packaged Rooftop Unit — Gas Heat / Electric Cooling — Installation, Operation and Maintenance Manual

## 1. Safety considerations

### 1.1 Purpose and qualified personnel

This manual covers installation, operation, inspection, and maintenance of Vantorr Climate Systems VRT Series packaged rooftop units with gas heating and electric cooling. The instructions apply to models VRT-036, VRT-048, and VRT-060. Each unit uses a single-stage scroll compressor, R-454B refrigerant, and a VCB-200 control board. Read this manual, the unit rating plate, and all labels furnished with the equipment before handling or energizing the unit. Keep the manual accessible to the building operator and servicing contractor.

Installation and service shall be performed by personnel trained in commercial heating, ventilation, air conditioning, electrical work, fuel-gas piping, and safe refrigerant practices. The installer is responsible for determining which codes, permits, inspection requirements, and site conditions apply. Where local requirements are more restrictive than these instructions, follow the applicable requirements and contact Vantorr Technical Support when equipment compatibility is uncertain.

> **WARNING:** Improper installation, adjustment, alteration, service, or maintenance can cause fire, explosion, electric shock, refrigerant injury, property damage, serious injury, or death. Do not operate equipment with a known unsafe condition.

> **WARNING:** Disconnect and lock out all electrical supplies before removing access panels or contacting electrical and moving components. More than one disconnecting means may be present. Confirm that all circuits are de-energized with an appropriate test instrument.

### 1.2 Principal hazards

- Electrical energy can remain present at an external service point even when a thermostat is satisfied. Never rely on the thermostat as an electrical disconnect.
- Rotating fans and the blower can start without warning when an automatic control calls for operation. Keep hands, clothing, and tools away from moving parts.
- Sheet-metal edges, coil fins, and roof openings can cause injury. Wear task-appropriate protection and guard open roof penetrations.
- Hot heat-exchanger surfaces, burners, and vent components may remain hazardous after a heating call ends. Allow components to cool before inspection.
- Fuel gas can accumulate if a joint leaks or combustion equipment operates improperly. Do not use an open flame to locate a leak.
- Refrigerant is stored under pressure. Uncontrolled release can injure the skin or eyes and can displace breathable air in an enclosed area.

### 1.3 R-454B and A2L refrigerant safety

R-454B is classified as an A2L refrigerant, indicating lower flammability under the applicable refrigerant safety classification. It must not be treated as a nonflammable refrigerant. A leak may create a potentially ignitable mixture under unfavorable conditions. Perform service using procedures, tools, leak-detection instruments, and recovery equipment suitable for R-454B and A2L refrigerants. Follow all current codes governing work on mildly flammable refrigerant systems.

> **WARNING:** Keep flames, sparks, smoking materials, and other ignition sources away from a suspected refrigerant release. If a leak is suspected, stop work that could produce ignition, protect personnel, ventilate the affected area by an approved method, and investigate only when conditions are safe.

Do not substitute another refrigerant, mix refrigerants, or introduce oxygen or compressed air into the sealed circuit. Use only an approved inert gas and procedures appropriate to the work when pressure testing is necessary. Recover refrigerant with compatible equipment before opening the circuit. Keep recovery containers identified and do not mix recovered refrigerant with a different type. Where brazing is permitted, establish the required safe work conditions, remove refrigerant from the section being serviced, and use appropriate inert-gas purging practices.

A2L precautions also apply to the indoor air path, ductwork, and occupied areas served by the unit. Do not defeat a refrigerant detection system or bypass its associated safety response. If a leak alarm or mitigation condition is indicated, follow Section 16 before attempting normal operation. Observe applicable requirements for leak detection, ventilation, electrical work, and ignition-source control.

### 1.4 Gas and combustion safety

> **WARNING:** If gas is smelled or a gas leak is suspected, do not operate switches or equipment that could ignite the gas. Keep people clear, isolate the gas supply only when it is safe to do so, and contact the gas supplier or emergency responders from a safe location.

Gas piping shall be installed and tested by qualified personnel. Verify fuel type against the rating plate before making connections. Do not adjust gas controls to compensate for incorrect fuel, poor combustion air, or an unsuitable vent arrangement. Never operate the heating section when the heat exchanger, burner compartment, combustion-air path, or vent outlet is damaged or blocked.

### 1.5 Handling and service rules

Protect safety devices and factory-installed wiring from physical damage. Reinstall access doors, seals, guards, and panels before leaving a unit in service. Do not substitute nonapproved control components or alter the designed safety sequence. Record the unit model, serial number, site location, observed operating condition, and work completed. When a condition cannot be verified safely with the information on the unit, stop and contact Vantorr Technical Support.

> **CAUTION:** Do not use the equipment as a construction heater or operate it while construction dust, paint vapor, or other contaminating materials are being drawn through the air system.

## 4. Receiving and rigging

### 4.1 Receiving inspection

Inspect the shipment before removing protective packaging. Examine the cabinet, base rails, coil guards, service panels, gas connection area, electrical entry, and exposed fittings for damage. Confirm that the nameplate model matches the purchase documentation and intended roof location. Look for signs of tipping or impact and report any concealed damage through the receiving process before installation work conceals the evidence.

1. Compare the unit identification and ordered configuration with the project equipment schedule.
1. Inspect all exterior surfaces and accessible compartments for transport damage or loose materials.
1. Verify that supplied accessories and installation materials have been accounted for.
1. Keep openings protected against rain, debris, and airborne construction material until connections are complete.
1. Record shortages, damage, and identifying information in the installation report.

Do not energize a damaged unit to determine whether it operates. If damage involves refrigerant piping, the gas train, electrical components, or safety devices, keep the equipment isolated until inspected by qualified personnel.

### 4.2 Planning the lift

Plan the lift around the actual unit weight, handling geometry, roof capacity, travel path, overhead restrictions, weather, and surrounding personnel. Use a qualified lifting contractor when required. Select rigging and lifting equipment based on the marked equipment configuration and the lifting arrangement provided with the unit. Account for any added shipping materials or field-installed accessories. Do not use service piping, coil guards, panels, or protruding components as attachment points.

> **WARNING:** Suspended loads can shift or fall. Establish an exclusion zone, use approved lifting points and spreader arrangements where required, and keep all personnel out from under the suspended equipment.

### 4.3 Lifting and positioning

1. Review the site-specific lift plan with the lifting crew and roofing contractor.
1. Confirm the roof curb is ready, square, properly supported, and free of obstructions.
1. Secure removable panels and protect exposed connections before hoisting.
1. Attach rigging only at designated load-bearing points shown on the unit or approved rigging instructions.
1. Raise the unit slowly and verify that it remains controlled and level.
1. Guide it into alignment without placing hands or feet between the unit base and curb.
1. Lower the base uniformly onto the curb gasket or approved support surface.
1. Verify full bearing contact, alignment of supply and return openings, and service access before releasing the rigging.

Never drag the equipment across the roof membrane. Avoid placing concentrated loads on unsupported roofing. After setting, inspect the cabinet and base for distortion. Confirm that service panels open fully and that the air openings have not been obstructed by curb members, roofing material, or shipping braces.

### 4.4 Storage before installation

> **NOTE:** The lifting crew is responsible for selecting a safe means of hoisting the actual equipment. Operating weight is only one input to the lift plan.

## 5. Roof curb and installation

### 5.1 Roof structure and curb location

Coordinate the selected location with the structural engineer, roofing contractor, duct installer, and service personnel. The roof must support the equipment, curb, ductwork, snow or environmental loading, and personnel during maintenance. Position the unit so service doors are reachable from a safe walking surface and roof drainage cannot concentrate beneath the cabinet. Consider noise transmission, outside-air quality, exhaust discharges, and access for component replacement.

Do not install the unit where its condenser discharge can be trapped beneath a canopy or recirculated into an adjacent intake. Avoid nearby walls and equipment that restrict condenser airflow. Maintain separation from incompatible roof exhausts and other ignition or contamination sources as required by applicable codes. Access shall be sufficient for safe gas, electrical, refrigerant, and blower service without removing permanently installed building work.

### 5.2 Support, level, and roof slope

The curb must present a stable, level mounting surface and properly support the entire designated unit base. Curb dimensions, opening positions, fastening details, and weatherproofing shall match the approved curb arrangement for the received unit. Do not infer curb dimensions from the model number. Maintain gasket continuity and prevent unsealed bypass between supply and return paths.

The unit is not approved for installation on roofs with a slope greater than 1/4 in. per foot unless a level curb is used.

A roof within the stated limit does not eliminate the need for a level, properly constructed curb. Check the bearing surface in both directions and correct irregularities before setting the unit. Do not level a unit using unstable field shims or materials that could compress, slip, or compromise weather sealing. Confirm that the condensate drain arrangement will operate as intended after the unit is secured.

> **CAUTION:** A twisted or poorly supported base can distort access doors, compromise drain performance, and increase vibration. Correct the support condition before connecting rigid piping or ductwork.

### 5.3 Service and airflow clearances

For VRT-036, VRT-048, and VRT-060, the required clearances are service side 36 in., condenser coil side 18 in., top 60 in. unobstructed. Keep these spaces clear during installation and throughout equipment service life. Clearance is measured to the nearest permanent obstruction in the relevant direction. Do not count a space as available if access is blocked by piping, guards, or another rooftop unit.

Service clearance supports safe removal of panels and access to electrical, gas, and air-moving components. Condenser-side clearance supports outdoor airflow. The top clearance limits interference with upward discharge and maintenance access. These minimums do not override more restrictive local fire, building, gas, or electrical working-space requirements. Where the site layout cannot meet the required clearances, relocate or redesign the installation rather than attempting to compensate with altered fan operation.

### 5.4 Duct connections and weather sealing

1. Verify supply and return openings against the approved curb and duct layout before cutting roof penetrations.
1. Remove shipping closures and construction debris from the air openings.
1. Connect ductwork so the unit cabinet does not carry unsupported duct loads.
1. Seal transitions against air leakage and water intrusion with materials appropriate for rooftop use.
1. Provide required thermal insulation, vapor control, and access openings for future inspection.
1. Confirm that the return path does not draw flue products, solvent vapors, roof exhaust, or other contaminants into occupied spaces.
1. Verify that duct openings and related spaces meet applicable A2L refrigerant requirements.

Use flexible connections where the approved design calls for vibration isolation. Do not block the filter rack, coil service area, or blower access with field ductwork. A return-air arrangement must permit timely filter changes without dismantling adjacent building systems. Protect roof penetrations against wind-driven rain and do not use the unit cabinet as the only roofing seal.

### 5.5 Final installation check

> **NOTE:** Follow the approved structural and roofing design for curb anchorage and weatherproofing. This manual does not assign field fastening sizes or roof load limits.

## 6. Condensate drain

### 6.1 Drain arrangement

The cooling coil produces condensate during operation. Route water from the factory drain connection to an approved disposal point without allowing backflow, air leakage, or water accumulation in the unit. Verify the installed unit's drain connection location before selecting fittings. Confirm that the roof curb and adjacent ductwork do not interfere with drain access or required slope.

Use piping materials suitable for condensate service and local environmental conditions. Support the drain independently so its weight is not carried by the cabinet connection. Provide a continuous fall toward the discharge point and avoid dips where sludge or ice can accumulate. The drain system shall meet applicable plumbing and building requirements for termination, cleanouts, and protection of the roof surface.

### 6.2 Trapping and priming

Determine whether the drain pan is under negative or positive air pressure during blower operation. Provide a trap arrangement appropriate to the actual pressure relationship and the installed drain configuration. Verify any required trap dimensions against unit instructions or the approved mechanical design; do not apply an assumed trap height based only on cabinet appearance. An improperly designed trap can prevent drainage, admit air, or cause water to overflow into ducts and occupied areas.

1. Inspect the drain pan and outlet for shipping debris and damage.
1. Connect the drain piping without stressing the factory drain fitting.
1. Install the required trap and service access in an accessible position.
1. Prime the trap as necessary before operating the cooling section.
1. Introduce clean water into the pan and confirm that it flows to the approved outlet.
1. Operate the indoor blower and verify that normal airflow does not interrupt drainage.
1. Check all joints and the surrounding roof for leakage.

> **CAUTION:** Do not use a condensate line as a structural support, and do not connect the drain to an arrangement that can expose the unit to sewer gas or standing water.

### 6.3 Freeze and overflow protection

Evaluate exposure to freezing weather. Protect or route vulnerable drain sections as permitted by local requirements; do not assume continuous heat from the unit will keep rooftop piping above freezing. Provide an accessible means to inspect and clear blockages. Where the building design requires an overflow safety device or secondary protection, install and test it without altering factory safety wiring.

At scheduled service, inspect the pan for biological buildup, corrosion, trapped debris, and cracked fittings. Clean with materials compatible with the pan, coil, and seals. Flush the piping and verify free discharge. If water appears near the blower or on the roof unexpectedly, correct the drainage fault before further cooling operation.

## 7. Gas piping and venting

### 7.1 Fuel verification and responsibility

The gas heating section must be supplied with the fuel specified on the unit rating plate. Verify compatibility with the site gas system before installation. Gas piping sizing, service regulator selection, shutoff arrangement, sediment management, and pressure testing are responsibilities of qualified gas personnel under applicable codes and the approved mechanical design. Do not use the nominal heating input alone to select pipe size or regulator settings.

> **WARNING:** Connecting the unit to an incorrect fuel source, operating outside the allowed inlet conditions, or bypassing gas safety controls can result in fire, explosion, carbon monoxide exposure, injury, or death.

The gas heating inputs for VRT-036, VRT-048, and VRT-060 are listed in Section 3. Determine available supply conditions from the serving gas system and compare them with the actual rating plate. If the fuel type or supply characteristics do not match, do not light the unit. Request an approved resolution before proceeding.

### 7.2 Field piping installation

Route the gas line to the factory gas connection without obstructing panels, condenser airflow, electrical access, or roof walkways. Provide suitable support and protection against movement, corrosion, vibration, and mechanical damage. Install the required manual shutoff at an accessible location. Use fittings and sealing materials approved for the supplied fuel. Provide required means of isolating the appliance for testing and service.

1. Confirm that gas service is isolated before opening the piping system.
1. Verify the factory connection and approved direction of entry on the received unit.
1. Size and support the field piping using the design load, available supply, pipe route, and governing code.
1. Install the required shutoff, service fittings, and provisions for gas-system maintenance.
1. Complete piping without using the appliance connection to carry bending or installation loads.
1. Test field joints by an approved nonflame method before introducing gas to the appliance.
1. Restore supply only after all test arrangements are removed or returned to their operating configuration.

> **CAUTION:** Isolate the appliance from any piping test that could expose its internal gas components to a pressure beyond their approved limits. Use the rating plate and applicable codes to determine the required test and isolation procedure.

### 7.3 Combustion air and vent discharge

The outdoor installation must allow the heating section to receive adequate combustion air and safely discharge combustion products. Keep factory air inlets, burner-compartment openings, and vent outlets unobstructed. Do not add field ducting to a factory outlet, redirect discharge, or enclose the combustion section unless Vantorr specifically approves the arrangement. The vent configuration provided with the unit is part of the listed heating system.

Locate equipment so vent products cannot enter building openings, nearby outdoor-air intakes, occupied roof areas, or adjacent equipment. Account for roof geometry, snow accumulation, prevailing winds, and surrounding structures as required by code. Do not treat the unit's cooling-service clearance as evidence that every vent separation requirement has been satisfied; combustion and building-code requirements may be more restrictive.

Inspect the vent path and heat exchanger before initial firing. Remove shipping material and debris. Look for damaged seals, displaced parts, corrosion, soot, evidence of recirculation, and restrictions. Confirm that the access doors and burner compartment are secured as intended during operation.

### 7.4 Initial gas checks

1. Verify the manual shutoff and service access are identifiable and reachable.
1. Confirm that fuel type and supply conditions match the rating plate.
1. Perform the required gas-leak checks with an approved detector or solution.
1. Verify that combustion-air and vent openings are clear.
1. Initiate a supervised heating demand using Section 12.
1. Observe ignition, stable burner operation, and the absence of abnormal odor or noise.
1. Conduct the combustion and venting checks required by local codes and the commissioning plan.
1. Record results and correct any abnormal condition before placing the unit in regular service.

Do not adjust the gas valve by eye or use flame appearance alone to approve combustion. Where instrumented verification is required, perform it with calibrated equipment and compare readings with the limits specified on the unit and applicable service information. If correct operation cannot be established, close the gas supply, isolate the unit, and obtain technical assistance.

### 7.5 Continued safe operation

Check the area around the combustion openings during routine visits. Do not store flammable materials, roofing solvents, or loose debris against the unit. Reinspect after nearby roofing or structural work, which may change clearances or introduce vent restrictions. If occupants report combustion odors or symptoms of possible exhaust exposure, remove the heating section from service and investigate under established safety procedures.

## 10. Thermostat and controls

### 10.1 Control system overview

The VCB-200 coordinates requests for cooling, gas heating, and indoor airflow with the unit's installed safety devices. The field thermostat or approved building control interface provides operating demands; it does not directly authorize bypassing protective functions. Identify the actual control interface through the wiring diagram and unit labels before making connections. Match thermostat function to the VRT's gas-heat, electric-cooling arrangement rather than a heat-pump arrangement.

The thermostat should be located where it senses representative return or occupied-zone conditions, not direct sunlight, roof heat, supply air, drafts, or nearby heat-producing equipment. Coordinate thermostat placement and sensing strategy with the project controls design. Avoid wiring arrangements that allow a controller and an external device to issue conflicting commands.

### 10.2 Control setup and verification

1. Confirm the thermostat or building controller is compatible with the installed equipment and field interface.
1. Review the VRT wiring figure and identify the approved control connections.
1. Install control wiring in accordance with the approved electrical design.
1. Set the control for the intended gas-heating and electric-cooling functions following its documentation.
1. Verify that the indoor blower can be commanded through the approved fan function.
1. Request cooling and observe the expected Section 11 sequence.
1. End the cooling request and verify an orderly stop.
1. Request heating and observe the expected Section 12 sequence.
1. Return the system to the desired occupied operating mode.

> **NOTE:** A valid thermostat demand may not immediately start equipment if a protective condition or the compressor anti-short-cycle delay is active.

### 10.3 Operating considerations

Do not use rapid setpoint changes to force repeated compressor starts. The specified anti-short-cycle delay is 5 minutes for all covered models. Allow the VCB-200 to complete the delay before concluding that a cooling demand has failed. A thermostat display indicating a call does not prove that the unit has accepted the request; verify the unit indication and actual component response.

If an external controller manages occupancy or fan operation, coordinate its scheduling with the approved VRT operating sequence. Do not substitute an accessory contact for a required safety input. In a suspected refrigerant detection event, safety responses take precedence over ordinary comfort calls. Refer to Section 16 and the control-board diagnostic reference before resuming service.

## 11. Sequence of operation — cooling

### 11.1 Cooling demand

A cooling call from the approved thermostat or building controller is received by the VCB-200. When the request is valid, required permissive conditions are satisfied, and no safety response prevents cooling, the control coordinates operation of the indoor blower, outdoor heat-rejection components, and single-stage scroll compressor. The refrigeration system absorbs heat from indoor air passing over the evaporator and rejects that heat outdoors. Condensate flows from the indoor coil to the drain system during moisture removal.

The compressor anti-short-cycle delay is 5 minutes. The controller may withhold a compressor start while this delay is active, even with a continuing cooling demand. Do not bypass the delay during normal startup or troubleshooting. Cooling is not approved below the specified minimum outdoor temperature of 35 °F. Verify outdoor conditions before attempting a cooling performance check.

### 11.2 Normal response to a cooling call

1. The thermostat or controller establishes a cooling request.
1. The VCB-200 evaluates required operating and safety conditions.
1. The indoor airflow path is established as required by the operating sequence.
1. The cooling components operate when the applicable permissives and compressor delay allow.
1. Indoor air passes through the filter and coil, and conditioned air enters the supply duct.
1. The condensate system removes collected moisture without overflow.
1. When the demand ends, the VCB-200 removes the cooling request and coordinates shutdown.

The exact order of individual component energization and protective action is determined by the installed control configuration and approved diagram. Do not infer unlisted timing or terminal behavior from this overview.

### 11.3 Cooling performance observations

Check that filters are clean, the indoor fan operates smoothly, supply and return ducts are not blocked, and outdoor airflow is unobstructed. Listen for unusual compressor or fan sounds. Confirm that condensate exits the drain and does not appear inside the cabinet. If cooling capacity appears low, first verify airflow, control demand, outdoor temperature, and cleanliness before attaching service instruments to the refrigerant circuit.

> **CAUTION:** Do not add refrigerant solely because the leaving-air temperature seems higher than expected. Use the correct model-specific charging chart after confirming stable operating conditions and indoor airflow.

If a safety event stops cooling, record the observed status before resetting or removing power. Diagnose the cause and verify the repair; repeated forcing of a call can conceal an intermittent fault.

## 12. Sequence of operation — gas heating

### 12.1 Heating demand

A heating call is received by the VCB-200 from the approved thermostat or controller. The control supervises the heating sequence in conjunction with the installed combustion and safety components. The combustion system establishes the required conditions for ignition, the gas burner lights when permitted, and the heat exchanger transfers heat to the indoor airstream. The indoor blower distributes heated air through the supply system.

The unit must never be fired with the combustion-air path obstructed, the vent outlet restricted, or suspected damage to the heat exchanger. Protective devices are integral to safe operation; a thermostat call does not override them.

### 12.2 Normal response to a heating call

1. The thermostat or control system requests gas heating.
1. The VCB-200 evaluates required safety and operating conditions.
1. The heating system establishes the appropriate combustion preparation sequence.
1. Ignition and flame supervision proceed through the factory safety system.
1. The indoor blower delivers heated air as coordinated by the controls.
1. The heating section continues operating while a valid demand remains and safety conditions are satisfied.
1. At the end of the demand, fuel flow is stopped and the control completes its normal shutdown functions.

This description is functional, not a substitute for the unit-specific electrical diagram or combustion service procedure. Do not assume an undocumented purge interval, ignition trial duration, blower delay, or gas valve adjustment. Any abnormal ignition behavior requires qualified diagnosis with the appropriate instruments.

### 12.3 Heating checks

During commissioning, observe the system through a full call and shutdown. Confirm that combustion products discharge outdoors without recirculation. Check for gas leaks using an approved method, listen for abnormal burner or fan noise, and inspect accessible heating components for evidence of overheating, soot, or leakage. Verify that supply airflow is not restricted. Inspect the filter rack and duct connections before concluding that a combustion component is defective.

> **WARNING:** A suspected venting failure, flame rollout, gas leak, or heat-exchanger breach is an unsafe condition. Stop the heating section, secure the gas supply when safe, and do not return the unit to service until the cause has been corrected.

### 12.4 Interrupted or unsuccessful operation

If ignition does not occur or a heating demand ends unexpectedly, observe and record the controller status. Check that the thermostat demand, power supply, gas availability, service shutoff, airflow path, and safety prerequisites are correct. Do not repeatedly cycle power to erase evidence of a fault. Use the *VRT Series Status LED Flash Codes* figure in Section 17 for the control's indicated diagnostic condition; do not assign a meaning from a different Vantorr board.

After correcting a verified cause, carry out a complete supervised heating test. If proper ignition, stable operation, or safe vent discharge cannot be demonstrated, leave the equipment isolated and contact Vantorr Technical Support.

## 14. Refrigerant charging

### 14.1 General charging precautions

The VRT refrigeration circuit contains R-454B. Determine the installed model before making charge decisions. Confirm indoor airflow, filter condition, outdoor coil cleanliness, operating mode, ambient conditions, and stable compressor operation before interpreting refrigerant measurements. Use calibrated, R-454B-compatible instruments and follow A2L safety practices in Section 1. A reading taken with poor airflow, an obstructed coil, or unstable operation can lead to an incorrect charge decision.

The factory charges shown in Section 3 are identification data, not instructions to add that amount to a charged unit. When the circuit has been opened or refrigerant has been lost, locate and correct the cause, perform approved integrity and evacuation procedures, and use the unit's authorized charging method. ## 16. Refrigerant detection system (A2L)

### 16.1 Purpose and inspection

The refrigerant detection provisions associated with the VRT's A2L application are safety features, not general-purpose cooling controls. Their purpose is to identify conditions requiring the specified protective response. Locate installed sensing devices, associated labeling, and the approved control interface before service. Do not move, cover, disable, or electrically bypass a sensor. Protect sensing locations from cleaning chemicals, coating materials, damage, and obstructed airflow.

> **WARNING:** A suspected refrigerant release must be treated as a potential flammability and exposure hazard even if the detection system has not indicated an alarm. Do not use the absence of an indication as proof that the area is safe.

### 16.2 Response to a detected condition

If the refrigerant detection system indicates a concern, protect the area and follow the equipment's documented mitigation response. The VCB-200 and associated components must remain connected as approved. Stop ordinary diagnostics that could introduce ignition. Keep occupants and personnel away from affected areas as needed and ventilate by methods authorized for the site. Do not defeat mitigation to restore comfort cooling or heating.

After conditions are made safe, inspect for the source of the release with compatible equipment. Repair and verify the sealed circuit under approved R-454B procedures. Confirm the detection system and its interface are intact before returning to service. For a sensor, communication, or mitigation fault that cannot be resolved from approved instructions, contact Vantorr Technical Support.

### 16.3 Periodic verification

During scheduled service, inspect sensor mounting, wiring protection, contamination, and any associated status indication. Perform only the functional checks allowed by the installed system's approved procedure. Record findings without introducing a test gas, bypass, or simulated condition unless the procedure explicitly authorizes it. Replace damaged parts only with approved components and verify normal operation afterward.

## 18. Maintenance schedule

### 18.1 Routine maintenance

Maintenance frequency may need to increase in dusty, corrosive, high-occupancy, or otherwise severe applications. Isolate electrical energy, control fan movement, and observe gas and A2L precautions before opening the unit. Reinstall all covers and verify normal operation after service.

Filters should be inspected monthly during the cooling season and replaced at least every 3 months.

The condenser coil must be cleaned at least once a year.

Replace filters with the exact sizes listed for the installed model and confirm every filter is secured against bypass. Investigate oil staining, water leakage, soot, loose components, or changes in sound rather than treating them as normal aging. Enter observations, corrective actions, and unresolved concerns in the maintenance log.

## 19. Troubleshooting

### 19.1 Systematic fault isolation

Begin with safe access, the actual operating demand, and the observed VCB-200 status. Confirm the model, nameplate supply, clean filters, intact ductwork, gas availability where applicable, and outdoor conditions. Inspect before replacing components. When refrigeration instruments are needed, use the matching Section 14 chart; when airflow is in question, use Section 15. Where an unsafe condition is suspected, stop the affected equipment rather than continuing diagnostic operation.

| Symptom | Possible cause | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No response to thermostat | No supply power or invalid demand | Verify disconnect, rating-plate supply, controller request, and status indication |
| Cooling demand but no compressor start | Anti-short-cycle delay or unmet safety permissive | Allow specified delay; see the Status LED Flash Codes figure |
| Cooling weak or uneven | Dirty filter, restricted airflow, fouled coil, or charge concern | Inspect air path; measure blower condition; consult model charging chart |
| Outdoor section noisy | Debris, airflow restriction, loose mounting, or mechanical damage | Isolate power; inspect fan area and cabinet; correct verified condition |
| Condensate overflows | Blocked drain, unprimed trap, improper piping, or poor leveling | Inspect pan, trap, piping slope, and curb support; water-test drain |
| Heating does not start | Unsatisfied safety condition, unavailable gas, or control demand issue | Verify gas shutoff, vent path, thermostat call, and controller indication |
| Heating stops unexpectedly | Air restriction, combustion concern, or protective operation | Check filters, blower, venting, and recorded status; conduct qualified gas checks |
| Abnormal combustion odor | Gas leakage or venting problem | Stop heating, secure area, and follow gas emergency procedure |
| Repeated safety response | Underlying operating fault or sensor concern | Record status; inspect affected system; avoid repeated resets |
| Refrigerant detection indication | Potential refrigerant release or detection-system fault | Follow Section 16; verify safety before leak investigation |
| High energy use or poor comfort | Control schedule, dirty surfaces, or duct leakage | Check operating demand, coil cleanliness, filters, and duct integrity |
| Water inside cabinet | Drain fault, cabinet leakage, or excessive condensation | Inspect drain, panel seals, duct insulation, and roof penetration |

### 19.2 Corrective action and return to service

Correct only a demonstrated fault. Do not adjust refrigerant charge until airflow and operating conditions are verified; do not adjust gas controls to conceal poor combustion air; and do not bypass protective wiring to suppress a status indication. After repair, close the cabinet, restore approved operating conditions, and observe a complete cycle of the affected function. Record readings against the relevant figure or rating-plate specification without inventing missing values.

> **WARNING:** If a fault involves electrical damage, uncontrolled gas release, compromised venting, a suspected refrigerant leak, or a defeated safety function, leave the unit safely isolated until a qualified technician verifies the repair.

When the available figures, rating plate, and inspection results do not identify a safe corrective action, provide Vantorr Technical Support with the model, serial number, observed condition, and documented checks. Retain a clear record for subsequent service personnel.
