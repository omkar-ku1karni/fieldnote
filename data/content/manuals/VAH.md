# Vantorr VAH Series Multi-Position Air Handler — Installation, Operation and Maintenance Manual

## 1. Safety considerations

### 1.1 Purpose and application

This manual provides installation, operation, inspection, and maintenance instructions for Vantorr Climate Systems VAH Series Multi-Position Air Handlers, models VAH-024, VAH-036, and VAH-048. These indoor units contain the air-moving and indoor heat-transfer components of a matched comfort-conditioning system. An approved electric heat accessory may be installed where the application requires supplemental or primary electric resistance heating. The VAH Series does not contain a compressor and does not operate as a stand-alone refrigeration system.

Read this manual before receiving, installing, starting, inspecting, or servicing the equipment. Keep a legible copy with the building equipment records. The unit rating plate, approved accessory instructions, job drawings, and the figures in this manual are part of the information needed to complete an installation. When a job drawing or accessory instruction differs from a general procedure in this manual, stop and resolve the conflict with Vantorr Technical Support before proceeding. Do not substitute a field assumption for a missing equipment rating.

These instructions are intended for qualified installation and service personnel. Personnel must be trained in electrical safety, sheet-metal and ductwork practices, condensate piping, and refrigerant-system service as applicable. Refrigerant work must be performed by personnel authorized under applicable regulations. Building operators may perform only those routine checks that can be completed without opening energized compartments or exposing moving parts.

### 1.2 Safety message conventions

A WARNING identifies a condition that could cause death or serious injury. A CAUTION identifies a condition that could damage equipment or property or cause minor injury. A NOTE provides information that helps prevent incorrect installation, unnecessary service, or inaccurate diagnosis. Observe safety labels on the installed equipment in addition to the messages below. A safety message is not a replacement for applicable codes or good trade practice.

> **WARNING:** Electrical shock, fire, or arc-flash hazard. Disconnect and lock out all power sources serving the air handler and any installed electric heat before removing access panels. Verify absence of voltage using an appropriately rated instrument. More than one disconnecting means may be present.

> **WARNING:** Moving blower components can cause severe injury. Do not reach into the blower compartment, bypass door protection, or operate the unit with an access panel removed. Allow all moving components to stop before inspection.

> **WARNING:** Electric heating elements and surrounding metal surfaces may remain hot after operation. Isolate power and allow components to cool before working inside the heat compartment.

> **CAUTION:** Improper lifting, support, or cabinet handling can deform the coil casing, misalign the blower, or damage refrigerant connections. Handle the unit by its structural cabinet, not by tubing, drain connections, wiring, or access-panel edges.

> **NOTE:** The air handler is rated for R-410A applications. The refrigerant designation in this manual does not authorize use with a different refrigerant or conversion of a matched system.

### 1.3 Responsibilities and regulatory requirements

The installing contractor is responsible for confirming that the selected model, supply arrangement, accessory configuration, location, and system match are suitable for the project. Install equipment in accordance with applicable building, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire, and energy codes, and with requirements imposed by the authority having jurisdiction. Follow local requirements for seismic restraint, structural attachment, service access, ceiling-space protection, condensate disposal, and electrical isolation where applicable.

Inspect the shipping carton and unit for visible damage before accepting or moving the equipment. Record any damage under the project's receiving procedure. After removing packaging, check the cabinet, drain pan, coil, blower mounting, electrical enclosure, and refrigerant connections for displacement or damage. Retain protective caps until the connections are ready for installation. Do not energize equipment that appears to have been damaged in shipment.

Confirm that the installed unit rating plate identifies the intended model and electrical supply. A model name alone does not establish the permitted accessory heat arrangement, electrical protection, or connected outdoor-unit match. Record the identifying data from the rating plate in the service record; do not use the specifications of a nearby unit as a substitute.

### 1.4 Safe work preparation

1. Notify the building operator and identify all systems affected by the planned shutdown.
2. Locate and identify each power source, including supplies associated with electric heat and auxiliary controls.
3. Apply the site's lockout and tagout procedure, then verify that hazardous electrical energy is absent.
4. Confirm that the blower is stationary and that any electric heat components have cooled.
5. Protect occupants, ceiling finishes, insulation, and stored materials from water, dust, sharp metal, and falling objects.
6. Use suitable lifting equipment, personal protective equipment, and a stable work platform for the installed location.
7. Before restoring power, replace all guards, access panels, electrical covers, and removed insulation.

Where refrigerant piping is opened, prevent refrigerant release, protect the tubing from moisture and contamination, and follow approved recovery and evacuation practices. Do not apply open flame or uncontrolled heat to a refrigerant-containing component. Do not pressure-test or charge the system using a value inferred from another model or from experience with unrelated equipment.

### 1.5 Installation conditions that require correction

Do not proceed with startup if the cabinet lacks adequate structural support, the return-air path is obstructed, the filter arrangement is incomplete, condensate cannot drain safely, an electrical supply is not confirmed, an access panel cannot be installed securely, or a required safety device has been bypassed. Correct the condition, document the result, and perform a new pre-start inspection. If an equipment-specific requirement is unclear, contact Vantorr Technical Support with the model and rating-plate information available.

## 2. Model number nomenclature

### 2.1 Model identification

The VAH designation identifies the Vantorr multi-position air-handler family. The model identifiers covered by this manual are VAH-024, VAH-036, and VAH-048. The size designation distinguishes the models and corresponds to the nominal capacity stated in Section 3; it is not a substitute for verified delivered capacity in the actual installation. Actual system performance depends on the approved equipment match, airflow, duct conditions, controls, and operating environment.

Use the complete model identification shown on the unit rating plate when ordering approved service items, requesting assistance, preparing an installation report, or assessing an accessory. Do not infer electrical configuration, accessory approval, cabinet revision, or blower characteristics from the size designation alone. If additional characters appear on a particular unit's identification label, retain them in the equipment record and refer to the corresponding approved unit documentation.

### 2.2 Component identification

The VAB-50 is the designated VAH Series air-handler control board. It coordinates applicable blower and electric-heat control functions with the installed control system. Its presence does not establish which accessory features are installed or enabled. Determine the actual configuration by examining the unit, its rating plate, approved accessory documentation, and the control-wiring figure in Section 7.

Each VAH air handler is an indoor component of a split refrigeration system using R-410A. It includes an indoor coil, a condensate collection arrangement, a blower assembly, and service-access provisions. Refrigerant compression and outdoor heat rejection occur in the separately matched outdoor equipment. A request to charge or diagnose the complete refrigeration circuit therefore requires the documentation for the actual matched outdoor unit as well as this manual.

### 2.3 Equipment records

Maintain an installation record identifying the installed model, rating-plate information, installation orientation, matching outdoor unit, electric heat accessory if fitted, control interface, filter arrangement, and location of disconnecting means. Record any approved accessory instructions that supplement this document. Keep the record near the air handler or in the building maintenance system so that future technicians do not need to infer the configuration from a thermostat display or cabinet appearance.

> **NOTE:** Model nomenclature is an identification aid, not a field sizing procedure. Confirm load calculations, matched-system approval, and airflow selection independently before installation.

## 4. Installation positions

### 4.1 Location and orientation

The air handler can be installed upflow, downflow, horizontal left or horizontal right.

Select the orientation that provides a continuous return-air path, a clear supply connection, proper condensate removal, safe refrigerant piping access, and access to all service panels. Confirm the intended airflow direction from the actual cabinet markings and installation layout. Do not determine airflow direction merely by the location of a refrigerant connection or drain fitting. Before positioning the cabinet, check the path for transporting the unit into the mechanical room or ceiling space and confirm that the completed assembly can be removed or serviced if required.

The mounting surface must be level and capable of carrying the installed load without twisting the cabinet. A pitched building floor or ceiling framing member is not an acceptable reference for setting cabinet level. Use supports designed for the actual structure. Protect the unit from water intrusion, construction debris, corrosive vapors, and locations where uncontrolled outdoor air could enter the return plenum. Do not place the cabinet where access panels are concealed by permanent construction.

### 4.2 Upflow installation

In an upflow installation, return air enters from the lower portion of the arrangement and conditioned air leaves toward the upper supply duct. Construct a stable support base that preserves the return opening and allows the cabinet to sit without rocking. When a return-air platform forms part of the installation, seal the joint between the platform and cabinet so that unfiltered room or cavity air cannot be drawn into the blower compartment.

1. Verify that the intended location meets the published front service-access requirement and local code provisions.
2. Check the support base for structural soundness and level bearing surfaces.
3. Set the cabinet in the correct airflow orientation without loading the drain fittings or refrigerant tubing.
4. Secure the cabinet using approved attachment methods suitable for the building structure.
5. Install the return-air transition, specified filters, and supply connection with service access preserved.
6. Route the condensate piping so that gravity drainage and future cleaning are possible.
7. Complete electrical and refrigerant connections using the procedures in the applicable sections before startup.

Inspect the entire return opening after the duct connection is complete. A return platform must not create a pocket that traps moisture or introduces dust downstream of the filters. Where the return path is through a building cavity, confirm that the arrangement is permitted by local code and that the cavity is properly constructed and sealed.

### 4.3 Downflow installation

In a downflow installation, the supply discharge is directed downward. Verify that the floor opening, stand, supply plenum, or other support arrangement is designed for the unit and complies with requirements for separation from adjacent materials. The stated side clearance to combustibles does not eliminate requirements applicable to duct penetrations, electric-heat accessories, fire-rated construction, or the supporting surface.

1. Confirm that the cabinet and approved accessories are suitable for the intended downflow arrangement.
2. Prepare the discharge opening and support so that the unit's load is carried by structural members rather than unsupported duct sheet metal.
3. Check that the coil and condensate collection arrangement are configured as specified for the selected position.
4. Place and secure the cabinet without distorting the blower or coil access openings.
5. Seal the supply and return transitions and maintain unobstructed access to the installed filters.
6. Inspect the drain slope and provide all required overflow protection before operating the unit.

If the location requires an orientation-specific pan, support, or transition that is not present, do not improvise a substitute. Obtain the appropriate approved installation guidance. Before energizing the unit, confirm that neither the drain piping nor any electrical accessory interferes with a floor penetration or fire-protection assembly.

### 4.4 Horizontal left and horizontal right installation

Horizontal installations are commonly used above ceilings, in mechanical lofts, and in low-profile service spaces. The cabinet must be independently supported so that its full operating weight does not rest on a connected duct, refrigerant line, drain, or suspended-ceiling system. Arrange supports to distribute the load and prevent cabinet twist. Provide a safe path for filter changes, drain cleaning, blower access, and removal of service panels. Maintain the published front access clearance at the finished installation, not merely during construction.

1. Verify the selected horizontal airflow orientation using the cabinet markings and project layout.
2. Confirm the required coil and condensate arrangement for that orientation before hanging the unit.
3. Position structural supports outside drain and service openings and secure them to approved framing.
4. Set the cabinet level in the directions needed for proper pan drainage without forcing the cabinet out of square.
5. Connect the supply and return ductwork without allowing duct weight to pull the cabinet out of level.
6. Install the primary condensate line, overflow provisions, and any required separate secondary pan.
7. Inspect the installation from the service side and verify that all panels can be removed safely.

The terms horizontal left and horizontal right refer to distinct approved field arrangements. Do not interchange condensate locations or assume that a pan configured for one arrangement will collect water correctly in the other. Use the actual cabinet and approved orientation instructions to confirm which openings and internal components are active.

> **WARNING:** A suspended or elevated air handler that is not independently supported can fall and cause serious injury or property damage. Do not rely on ductwork, refrigerant tubing, or ceiling grid components to carry the unit.

> **NOTE:** The approved orientation determines condensate collection requirements. Always verify the installed pan arrangement before running the blower or introducing cooling condensate.

## 5. Condensate and secondary drain pan

### 5.1 Condensate system requirements

The cooling coil removes moisture from the return air when its surface temperature is below the air's dew point. Water collected at the coil must enter the intended drain pan and leave the cabinet through a properly installed drain connection. A drain system that appears satisfactory while the blower is off may back up or draw air through the drain when the blower operates. Design the piping for the pressure conditions present at the drain connection, the selected installation position, and the applicable plumbing code.

Use corrosion-resistant, code-accepted drain materials compatible with the installation environment. Support piping independently from the cabinet fitting. Avoid imposing bending loads on a molded or thin-wall pan connection. Maintain positive drainage toward an approved disposal point and do not route water where it may damage finishes, electrical equipment, stored goods, or occupied spaces. Provide an accessible method of inspecting and cleaning the primary line. Prevent condensate piping from obstructing filter removal, the electrical compartment, and the required service area.

### 5.2 Primary drain installation

1. Confirm which drain outlet is designated for the primary drain in the selected cabinet orientation.
2. Inspect the outlet and pan for packing debris, damage, and foreign material.
3. Connect the primary drain using a fitting and sealing method compatible with the pan material; do not overtighten.
4. Provide a trap where required by the operating pressure at the pan. Determine trap geometry from the installed pressure condition and approved installation details, not from an unrelated air handler.
5. Route the line continuously toward its approved point of discharge and install supports that prevent sagging.
6. Provide a cleanout or removable access arrangement at a location that can be reached after construction.
7. Fill or prime the trap as required for the installed arrangement, then test water flow with the blower both off and operating.
8. Check all fittings, joints, and the discharge point for leaks, backup, siphoning, or air ingestion.

A trap is a functional component of the drainage design, not simply a bend in the piping. Incorrect arrangement can restrict drainage or permit the blower to pull air through the line. Do not add a second, unplanned trap or combine drains in a way that defeats the intended pressure seal. Protect drain piping from freezing wherever local conditions make freezing possible, using a code-compliant method suitable for the site.

### 5.3 Overflow protection and secondary pan

A secondary drain pan is required when the unit is installed above a finished ceiling.

The secondary pan must be positioned to collect water escaping the air handler or its primary drain connections and must be supported independently so that accumulated water cannot deform the ceiling or place unintended loads on the cabinet. Route the secondary pan drain to a permitted, observable discharge location in accordance with local code. Do not conceal the secondary discharge where a persistent overflow would remain unnoticed. Keep the secondary pan and its outlet accessible for inspection and cleaning.

An auxiliary connection provided on the air-handler drain assembly is not automatically a substitute for a separate pan required by the installation location. An auxiliary outlet, a separate containment pan, and a condensate overflow safety device serve different purposes and may be required together. The authority having jurisdiction and approved installation documents determine the acceptable combination. Where a field-installed overflow switch is required or provided, follow its approved instructions and verify its operation without defeating the system's protective function.

> **WARNING:** Condensate overflow can cause ceiling collapse, electrical hazards, and extensive property damage. Install required secondary containment and overflow protection before operating the cooling system.

### 5.4 Drainage verification and recurring inspection

Pour clean water into the appropriate collection area using a method that does not wet electrical components, blower insulation, or the filter rack. Observe the water entering the primary line and exiting the approved discharge. Start the blower under controlled conditions and repeat the observation. Verify that the trap remains effective and that water does not accumulate, splash into the airstream, or enter the secondary pan during normal drainage. If an overflow control is fitted, test it using the manufacturer's approved method and restore the system to normal before leaving.

Inspect the pan and drain at cooling-season startup, whenever a filter or coil condition suggests restricted airflow, and after any reported water event. Remove biofilm, sediment, or construction debris using cleaning methods compatible with the pan and tubing. Do not use compressed air or cleaning chemicals in a manner that can rupture fittings, aerosolize contaminants into occupied space, or damage the coil. Record a recurring blockage as a system condition requiring investigation, rather than relying on repeated clearing alone.

> **CAUTION:** Do not plug an active drain outlet, connect an overflow drain to a concealed location, or leave a drain test incomplete after opening the system. Restore all drain paths and protective devices before returning the unit to service.

## 6. Ductwork

### 6.1 General air-distribution requirements

Supply and return ductwork must be designed to deliver the airflow required by the matched system while keeping external static pressure within the operating range shown in the appropriate blower performance figure. Air-handler nominal capacity does not define the installed airflow by itself. Duct geometry, fittings, terminal devices, filtration, grilles, dampers, and any installed accessory all affect resistance. A restrictive system can reduce heating and cooling performance, increase noise, promote coil icing, and activate protective controls.

Use approved duct materials, insulation, sealants, and supports appropriate for the building's occupancy and applicable code. Keep the return path free of contaminants from garages, chemical storage, open combustion appliances, or other locations prohibited as return-air sources. Do not use an attic, ceiling void, or framing cavity as a return path unless specifically permitted and properly constructed. Prevent loose insulation and construction debris from reaching the blower or coil.

### 6.2 Return-air connection and filtration

The filter system is an integral part of air-handler protection. Provide the exact filter quantity and size specified for the installed model in Section 3. A replacement filter must fit the rack tightly, allow the access door to close, and meet the application's approved filtration requirements. Filters must be installed in the designated direction of airflow. Do not leave gaps at the frame, remove a filter to increase airflow, or operate the system without a complete filter set.

1. Inspect the return duct and filter opening for construction debris and sharp edges.
2. Install a rigid, supported filter rack or the approved cabinet filter arrangement.
3. Seal return-side joints to prevent unfiltered air from bypassing the filters.
4. Confirm that the filter door or panel can be opened without removing permanent ductwork.
5. Insert all specified filters and verify that each seats against the intended stop.
6. Start the blower and check for door movement, bypass leakage, whistling, or filter distortion.

Account for filter resistance when evaluating external static pressure. A filter that fits dimensionally may still be inappropriate if its resistance prevents the selected blower arrangement from meeting the system's requirements. Compare measurements and selection data under representative installed conditions, and repeat measurements after any major filtration change.

### 6.3 Supply-air connection

Connect the supply plenum without twisting the cabinet or obstructing the coil outlet. Provide a smooth transition where the duct is larger or smaller than the cabinet connection. Avoid placing a severe restriction, abrupt elbow, or unsupported heavy fitting directly against the discharge where it would cause uneven airflow or transmitted vibration. Use flexible connections where suitable to isolate vibration, while maintaining required structural support and fire integrity.

Insulate and seal ductwork as required to control heat gain or loss, condensation, and air leakage. Seal penetrations in the air-handler cabinet only with compatible methods that do not cover identification labels, block drains, or prevent panel removal. If electric heat is installed, ensure that downstream materials and duct transitions comply with the heat accessory's approved application requirements. The cabinet's stated side clearance to combustibles does not certify all field-fabricated duct or heat-accessory conditions.

### 6.4 Balancing and external static pressure

Verify airflow after the entire duct network, grilles, filters, and accessories are installed. Measure external static pressure at appropriate accessible locations using a calibrated instrument and the accepted field method for the completed configuration. Record the locations used so future technicians can reproduce the test. Read the model-specific chart in Section 9 and identify whether the actual operating point is within the published performance data for the selected blower configuration.

If the intended airflow cannot be obtained, investigate the duct system before selecting a more aggressive blower setting. Check return and supply restrictions, dampers, filter loading, blocked grilles, poorly formed transitions, and leakage. A blower selection cannot correct a fundamentally undersized return duct or a closed balancing damper. Confirm the final setting under actual operation and record the measured condition in the commissioning report.

> **NOTE:** Always evaluate blower performance with the installed filters and normal duct accessories in place. Measurements made with service panels removed or filters omitted do not represent normal operation.

## 7. Electrical connections and electric heat

### 7.1 Electrical installation requirements

Electrical work must comply with the unit rating plate, the VAH Series Control Wiring Diagram and Electrical Data figure, approved accessory documentation, and applicable electrical code. Before wiring, identify the actual model and supply configuration. Verify that the building source matches the listed unit configuration and that the available electrical service is appropriate for any approved electric-heat accessory. Use only conductor material, insulation, routing methods, disconnecting means, and protective devices permitted by the applicable requirements.

A qualified electrician must establish conductor sizing, protective-device selection, and grounding or bonding arrangements. Never select a fuse, breaker, or conductor solely from the air handler's nominal capacity. Keep field wiring protected from sharp cabinet edges, moving blower components, refrigerant tubing, and hot heating surfaces. Use approved strain reliefs and close unused cabinet openings by an accepted method that preserves the enclosure's integrity.

The *VAH Series Control Wiring Diagram and Electrical Data* figure is used to verify the installed supply, allowable field connections, and electrical requirements; read the drawing legend and identification labels for the exact unit configuration before making or testing any connection.

> **WARNING:** Incorrect field wiring or electrical protection can cause fire, equipment damage, or electric shock. Follow the wiring figure and the installed rating plate. Do not use a connection arrangement copied from another unit.

### 7.2 Power and grounding

1. Open, identify, and lock out every relevant disconnecting means.
2. Confirm the model and electrical information on the unit rating plate.
3. Compare the available supply and applicable code requirements with the electrical data in the figure above.
4. Route approved conductors through designated field-entry provisions with appropriate protection.
5. Establish the equipment grounding and bonding arrangement required by code and equipment labeling.
6. Make connections exactly as shown for the actual installed configuration, maintaining separation of circuits where required.
7. Check mechanical security, insulation condition, and enclosure clearances without placing stress on device connections.
8. Replace electrical covers and verify that the unit can be isolated safely before the initial energization.

Do not use the cabinet sheet metal or a refrigerant tube as an intentional current-carrying path. Correct any loose enclosure fitting, damaged conductor insulation, missing grounding provision, or evidence of overheated electrical material before restoring power. Measure and document the supply condition using qualified test practices. If the measured condition is inconsistent with the unit's approved electrical data, stop the startup procedure and investigate the source.

### 7.3 Control wiring and VAB-50 interface

The VAB-50 control board receives permitted operating requests and coordinates supported air-handler functions. Available control connections and safety interfaces depend on the installed configuration. Use the wiring figure to identify the applicable connections and distinguish factory wiring from field wiring. Do not infer terminal function from the appearance of a neighboring board, replace a required safety connection with a temporary bypass, or apply an unverified external voltage to a control input.

Route field control cables separately from high-energy wiring as required by code and approved installation practice. Protect conductors from abrasion at cabinet entries. Label field cables in the building documentation rather than relying on unrecorded assumptions. Check that all required doors, interlocks, and protective devices are installed before commissioning. If an interface is not shown for the installed unit or accessory, request approved instructions from Vantorr Technical Support before connecting it.

### 7.4 Electric heat accessories

An electric heat accessory may be used only when specifically approved for the installed VAH model and application. Confirm the accessory identification, installed rating, supply requirements, cabinet fit, airflow requirements, and any additional installation clearances from the approved accessory instructions and the actual equipment labels. Do not assume that heat accessories are interchangeable among VAH-024, VAH-036, and VAH-048. The air handler can also be installed without electric heat when the system design does not require it.

Before installing an approved accessory, isolate all power and inspect the intended mounting area for shipping material, damaged insulation, or foreign objects. Mount the accessory using its supplied approved method, protect wiring from contact with heating elements, and keep the entire required airflow path unobstructed. Retain all original safety controls and guards. The heat accessory's temperature protection is a safeguard, not a substitute for proper airflow or correct duct construction.

### 7.5 Electric heat checkout

1. Confirm the accessory is approved for the actual unit and is mechanically secured.
2. Verify the accessory's electrical supply and protective arrangements against its labeling and the applicable electrical data.
3. Inspect the blower path, filters, supply duct, and return duct for restrictions.
4. Verify that all heat-compartment covers, safety devices, and cabinet panels are installed.
5. Restore power under the site's controlled startup procedure.
6. Initiate a permitted heat demand using the installed control system.
7. Confirm that the blower operates and that heating operation occurs in the manner specified for the installed accessory and controls.
8. End the demand and observe a complete normal stop without opening the cabinet while energized.
9. Record any abnormal odor, unusual noise, safety intervention, or temperature concern and correct it before handover.

A brief odor from a recently handled heating compartment may have causes other than equipment failure, but any persistent smoke, scorching, arcing, or insulation damage is an immediate shutdown condition. Do not repeatedly reset a heat safety control in an attempt to complete startup. Investigate duct restriction, filter loading, airflow selection, and the heater installation before returning the system to service.

> **CAUTION:** A successful blower run does not establish that electric heat is correctly protected or configured. Complete the separate accessory checks whenever heat is installed.

## 8. Refrigerant detection sensor

### 8.1 A2L-ready provision and present refrigerant

The VAH Series units described in this manual are specified for R-410A. The term A2L-ready refers to provisions that may support a separately approved refrigerant-detection and mitigation arrangement in a compatible future or application-specific configuration. It does not mean that the air handler, as installed, is approved for any alternative refrigerant. It does not authorize refrigerant conversion, substitution of an outdoor unit, or installation of a detector without the relevant equipment approval.

A refrigerant detection sensor is intended to recognize a condition identified by an approved detection system and report it to associated controls. Where an approved mitigation arrangement is provided, the controls may direct protective equipment functions according to the instructions for that arrangement. The actual permitted behavior, device placement, verification method, and equipment compatibility must be obtained from the approved sensor and system documentation. This manual intentionally does not specify a detection threshold, response timing, or reset sequence.

### 8.2 Installation and handling considerations

Do not assume a detection sensor is installed merely because the VAB-50 control board or cabinet contains a provision for one. Before undertaking work involving a sensor, verify its presence, applicable approval, mounting arrangement, and instructions for the complete system. Keep a fitted sensing element unobstructed and protected from dust, cleaning spray, liquid water, and physical impact. Do not paint, tape over, relocate, or disconnect it to make other work easier.

If the sensor or its associated control indicates a condition requiring mitigation, follow the approved system instructions and the site's refrigerant-safety procedure. Keep occupants and service personnel clear of any area identified by applicable safety guidance. A detector indication is not a substitute for investigation by a qualified technician, and a return to normal display does not prove that the source of an indication has been corrected.

### 8.3 Inspection and service boundaries

At routine service, inspect the accessible sensor installation, if present, for dirt, damage, water exposure, and unsecured mounting. Use only the verification and service procedure provided for the approved sensor arrangement. Do not introduce refrigerant into an occupied space as an informal sensor test. Do not bypass a detection input or defeat a mitigation function to keep the system running. If a sensor-related indication is present, document the observed condition and contact Vantorr Technical Support when the applicable approved instructions do not resolve it.

> **WARNING:** An unapproved refrigerant conversion or disabled protective detection system may create a hazardous installation. Use only the refrigerant and safety configuration approved for the exact matched equipment.

> **NOTE:** No sensor installation, conversion, or commissioning instruction in this section supersedes the R-410A specification for the VAH models listed in Section 3.

## 9. Blower performance

### 9.1 Using the performance information

Blower performance must be evaluated for the exact model and installed configuration. The published figures relate blower operating selections to the air-system resistance encountered at the unit. They are not a replacement for a duct design or a field measurement. Use a calibrated instrument to determine external static pressure under representative conditions with the complete filter set, supply system, return system, and applicable accessories in place. Observe the chart's labels and any qualifications printed within the figure.

Select the airflow requirement from the approved matched-system design and applicable accessory instructions. Find the operating point in the appropriate performance figure, then use only a selection supported by that figure. If the measured resistance or required performance falls outside its published information, correct the installation or obtain application guidance rather than extending the chart by guesswork. Record both the measured condition and final selection for future service.

## 10. Sequence of operation

### 10.1 General control behavior

The VAB-50 coordinates air-handler responses to requests from the installed control system. The sequence described here is functional and does not replace the wiring figure, accessory instructions, or approved matched-system control documentation. Actual permitted functions depend on equipment fitted to the cabinet. Protective controls may interrupt an operating request when an unsafe condition or incompatible state is detected.

At startup, verify that the control system is correctly configured for the actual indoor and outdoor equipment without assuming the settings from a previous job. An air-handler response cannot by itself confirm that a separately installed outdoor unit is operating correctly. The indoor blower and outdoor refrigeration equipment must be evaluated as components of the complete matched system.

### 10.2 Continuous fan request

When an authorized fan request is present, the control system calls for indoor air circulation without necessarily calling for cooling or electric heat. The VAB-50 responds according to the supported blower arrangement. Air travels through the installed return filter, across the indoor air path, and into the supply duct. The available blower selection and any interaction with an active heating or cooling request are determined by the approved control arrangement. When the request ends, observe the normal stop behavior for the installed equipment.

### 10.3 Cooling request

On a valid cooling request, the controls coordinate indoor blower operation with the matched outdoor cooling equipment. Air passing over the indoor coil is cooled and may release moisture into the condensate pan. The condensate drain must remain effective throughout operation. If an approved overflow protective device interrupts the request, investigate and correct the drainage condition before resetting or returning the system to service.

During cooling, check that filters are installed, supply and return paths remain open, and the blower operates according to the selected performance data. Persistent coil frost, water carryover, or inadequate delivered cooling requires diagnosis of airflow and the complete refrigeration system. Do not attempt to correct such a condition by adding refrigerant without first establishing its cause under the outdoor equipment's approved service procedure.

### 10.4 Electric heating request

Where approved electric heat is installed, a valid heating request permits the applicable controls to operate the accessory with required blower operation and protective supervision. Heating elements transfer heat to air flowing through the cabinet. Safety controls protect against abnormal conditions but are not intended to compensate for a blocked duct, missing filters, incorrect accessory, or inadequate airflow. Observe operation through a complete call using safe external measurement methods.

If electric heat is not installed, do not interpret the absence of heat output as an air-handler failure. Confirm the system design and the intended source of heating. For installations with separately matched heat-pump equipment, follow the applicable outdoor-unit instructions for its heating operation. This manual does not establish reversing-valve behavior or thermostat configuration for that equipment.

### 10.5 Protective interruption and shutdown

The installation may include an overflow safety device, heater safety controls, control-system protection, or an approved refrigerant-detection interface. A protective interruption may prevent a requested function from operating. Do not defeat the protection to complete a test. Determine whether the interruption originates in the air handler, an accessory, the field controls, or the separately matched outdoor equipment.

When a demand ends, the installed controls manage the applicable stop sequence. Do not use an assumed timing interval as a pass or fail test. Instead, observe whether the system completes the approved sequence without noise, overheating, water leakage, or unintended operation. Record deviations and investigate before leaving the equipment unattended.

## 11. Control board diagnostics

### 11.1 VAB-50 status indication

The VAB-50 provides a status LED for service observation. Status interpretation must be made from the published figure, not from a technician's recollection of another control board. Make observations only through a safe viewing method that does not require exposure to energized conductors or moving blower components. If access conditions are unsafe, isolate power and arrange an approved diagnostic method.

The *VAH Series Status LED Flash Codes* figure is used to interpret the VAB-50 indicator; observe the complete indication on the installed board, then match it directly to the corresponding entry in the figure without assuming a pattern or meaning.

[[FIGURE:VAH-LED]]

### 11.2 Diagnostic procedure

1. Confirm the model, operating request, and reported symptom with the building operator.
2. Check accessible supply, disconnect, control, and external safety conditions using safe test practices.
3. Observe the VAB-50 status indication without bypassing covers or protective devices.
4. Use the Status LED Flash Codes figure to identify the appropriate published diagnostic direction.
5. Inspect the affected subsystem using the wiring figure, approved accessory instructions, and measured operating conditions.
6. Correct the verified cause and restore the installation to its approved configuration.
7. Reapply the operating request and confirm that the original symptom does not recur.
8. Record the original observation, corrective work, and final functional checks.

A control-board indication is a diagnostic starting point. It does not independently establish that the board is defective or that the reported problem originated in the cabinet. Verify field controls, wiring integrity, airflow, drainage, and accessory conditions before considering replacement of any control component.

> **WARNING:** Do not bridge safety devices or probe an energized board with loose conductors to force an output. Follow qualified electrical diagnostic procedures and preserve all protective functions.

## 12. Maintenance

### 12.1 Maintenance program

A regular maintenance program is necessary to preserve airflow, drainage, comfort, and serviceability. Adjust inspection frequency to the actual environment, operating duty, filter loading, and building maintenance plan. Equipment serving dusty or heavily occupied areas may require more frequent attention than equipment in clean, controlled spaces. Keep records of observations rather than recording only that the equipment was serviced.

| Component | Inspection occasion | Check | Required action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Return air filters | At routine service and when airflow is reduced | Loading, fit, damage, bypass | Replace with specified quantity and size as needed |
| Indoor coil | Before cooling operation and when performance declines | Dirt, obstruction, frost evidence, water carryover | Clean by approved method; correct source of contamination |
| Primary drain and trap | Before cooling operation and after water complaints | Flow, blockage, leaks, trap function | Clean, test, and restore drainage |
| Secondary pan and drain | At routine cooling checks and after any overflow | Standing water, corrosion, outlet obstruction | Remove water, correct cause, and verify discharge |
| Blower and cabinet | At seasonal inspections or abnormal noise | Debris, vibration, mounting, panel fit | Clean and correct verified defects |
| Electrical enclosure | During qualified scheduled service | Loose or damaged material, heat evidence, enclosure condition | Isolate power and repair by approved method |
| Electric heat, if installed | Before heating operation | Airflow path, guards, signs of overheating | Correct problems and perform controlled operation test |
| Sensor provision, if equipped | As required by approved sensor instructions | Obstruction, damage, installation condition | Follow the approved sensor service procedure |
| Supply and return ductwork | At seasonal review and after alterations | Leakage, blocked openings, damaged insulation | Repair and rebalance as required |

### 12.2 Filter replacement

1. Turn off the operating request and isolate power when access could expose moving parts.
2. Open the designated filter access without bending the frame or dislodging insulation.
3. Remove the existing filters carefully to avoid depositing collected dust in the cabinet.
4. Inspect the rack, seals, and adjacent return opening for bypass or debris.
5. Install the model's specified filter quantity and dimensions as listed in Section 3.
6. Check the indicated airflow direction and verify that each filter is fully seated.
7. Close the access panel and confirm that it seals securely during blower operation.

Do not wash a disposable filter or substitute an undersized filter with field-added filler material. If a different filtration specification is required for the building, confirm its compatibility with the required blower operating point before making the change. Retest airflow when the new arrangement materially changes resistance.

### 12.3 Coil, blower, and condensate cleaning

Isolate power before opening the cabinet. Protect wiring, insulation, and controls from cleaning water and chemical residue. Remove dry debris using suitable methods that do not deform coil fins or drive contamination deeper into the coil. Use cleaning agents only where compatible with the coil, pan, seals, and surrounding materials. Rinse or remove residues according to the approved cleaner procedure. Do not expose the blower motor or control board to direct spray.

Inspect the blower wheel and housing for accumulated dirt that could impair performance or balance. Remove contamination without bending wheel components or changing the factory alignment. Confirm that guards, supports, and access panels are secure before energizing the blower. Inspect the primary pan, drain opening, trap, and secondary pan while the compartment is accessible. Follow the flow-test procedure in Section 5 after cleaning.

### 12.4 Electrical and heat maintenance

A qualified technician should inspect accessible electrical components with all sources safely isolated. Look for damaged insulation, unusual discoloration, loose supports, water entry, and signs of overheating. Make repairs using approved practices and components, then restore covers before operation. For a fitted electric heat accessory, check the air path, protective devices, element enclosure, and accessory mounting in accordance with its approved instructions. Investigate any prior protective trip rather than simply restoring operation.

### 12.5 Seasonal return-to-service inspection

Before the cooling season, confirm filter condition, condensate drainage, cabinet integrity, blower operation, matched-system response, and the absence of water leakage. Before the heating season, verify the installed heating arrangement, airflow path, and approved heat-accessory operation if present. After any significant duct alteration or change in filtration, repeat airflow evaluation against Section 9. After any maintenance requiring disconnection of refrigerant piping, complete the full matched-system service procedure before returning the system to normal use.

> **NOTE:** Record the installation condition, maintenance performed, unresolved issues, and any resulting change in airflow selection. A repeatable service history helps distinguish new faults from long-standing installation deficiencies.

## 13. Troubleshooting

### 13.1 Diagnostic approach

Begin with the reported symptom and the actual operating request. Establish whether the fault is within the VAH cabinet, the installed controls, an approved accessory, ductwork, drainage, or the separately matched outdoor equipment. Use appropriate instruments and record what was measured. A thermostat indication, control LED, or occupant report is useful evidence, but none replaces inspection of the affected system. Refer to the electrical and LED figures for details that are not stated in this text.

| Symptom | Possible cause | Verification | Corrective action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blower does not operate | Supply interruption, control issue, protective device | Confirm safe electrical conditions, operating request, and published LED interpretation | Restore verified supply or control fault; resolve protection before restart |
| Blower runs but airflow is weak | Loaded filters, restricted duct, unsuitable selection | Inspect filters and dampers; measure external static pressure; use model blower figure | Remove restriction and verify approved blower selection |
| Uneven room temperatures | Duct leakage, closed outlets, balancing issue | Inspect air distribution and compare delivered conditions | Repair ductwork and rebalance system |
| Cooling request produces little cooling | Airflow problem or fault in matched refrigeration equipment | Check blower, coil condition, and outdoor system using its documentation | Correct verified air-side or matched-system fault |
| Ice or frost appears on indoor coil | Airflow restriction or refrigeration-system issue | Check filters, blower, duct resistance, and outdoor service findings | Correct cause before resuming cooling |
| Water leaks near cabinet | Blocked drain, ineffective trap, pan misalignment | Inspect pan, trap, slope, and flow under blower operation | Restore drainage and any required secondary protection |
| Secondary pan contains water | Primary drain failure or cabinet water escape | Trace water source and verify primary line | Repair cause, dry affected area, and retest |
| Electric heat does not operate | No approved heat accessory, control issue, safety interruption | Confirm fitted accessory, demand, and approved electrical checks | Correct verified issue; do not bypass heat safety |
| Heat compartment shows overheating | Restricted airflow or heat-accessory defect | Shut down; inspect filters, duct, blower, and approved accessory | Resolve fault and verify safe heat operation |
| Unexpected noise or vibration | Loose panel, duct stress, blower debris, support issue | Inspect mounting and air path with power safely isolated | Secure or repair affected components |
| Repeated electrical interruption | Wiring or equipment fault | Have qualified personnel inspect the circuit and equipment data | Correct fault before reenergizing |
| Sensor-related protective indication | Approved detection arrangement requires investigation | Follow installed sensor and system instructions | Preserve mitigation and obtain qualified service |

### 13.2 When the blower will not run

Verify the building's intended operating mode and whether a valid fan or conditioning request is present. Confirm the appropriate disconnects are in their normal operating condition without bypassing a tripped protective device. Use the VAB-50 status indication and its published figure to determine the next diagnostic step. If the controls request operation but the blower remains off, isolate electrical power and inspect accessible connections, motor assembly condition, and applicable protective interfaces according to the wiring figure. Do not condemn a board or motor solely because the fan is stationary.

### 13.3 When comfort or airflow is inadequate

Confirm that every required filter is installed and in serviceable condition. Check for closed grilles, blocked returns, dampers moved during construction, collapsed flexible sections, and leakage near the cabinet. Measure external static pressure with panels and filters installed, then consult the exact model blower performance figure. If the airflow is adequate but comfort remains poor, verify the matched outdoor equipment, thermostat location, control requests, and building load conditions using the applicable system documentation. Do not compensate for a refrigeration fault by making an unsupported blower selection.

### 13.4 When water or ice is observed

Stop cooling when water threatens building finishes or when coil frost is visible. Protect the area below an elevated unit. Inspect drain connection integrity, pan orientation, cabinet level, trap function, and flow to the approved discharge. Clean and retest the drain before restoring cooling. Where icing is present, confirm airflow and filter condition before investigating the complete refrigeration system. Do not operate with a known blocked drain or rely on a secondary pan for routine water removal.

### 13.5 Service completion and escalation

After any repair, restore all factory and approved field safety provisions, close panels, and perform a controlled functional check in the operating mode associated with the original complaint. Check condensate flow during cooling and heat-accessory operation where fitted. Document the symptom, observed status indication, measurements, actions, and final outcome. If the required information is absent from the rating plate or figures, or if a safety concern remains unresolved, leave the affected operation disabled and contact Vantorr Technical Support with the complete unit identification and service observations.

> **WARNING:** Repeatedly resetting a protective device without identifying its cause may expose personnel and property to electrical, thermal, or water damage. Correct the underlying condition before returning the equipment to service.
